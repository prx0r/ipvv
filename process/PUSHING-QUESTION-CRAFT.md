# How to ask a good pushing question

*2026-10-31. Learned by writing bad ones first, then reading the user's actual questions
in the logicvid sessions and rewriting in that voice. This is the process note for the
next agent — how to go from "formal academic framing" to "the question that makes the
text squirm."*

## What I did wrong first

My first Bhairavastava questions were:
- "If svātmamaya = constitutively Bhairava's, who bows?" ← formal, essay prompt
- "How can suffering be acknowledged AND fear be absolutely absent?" ← Venn diagram
- "If identity always (v2), why nondifference conditional on hymn (v7)?" ← logic puzzle

These read like seminar questions. They're technically correct but they DON'T SOUND LIKE
SOMEONE WHO WANTS TO KNOW. They frame the tension for the reader instead of letting the
reader feel the tension.

## What the user's actual questions sound like

From the logicvid sessions:
- "is everything rooted in love? then what is FEAR?"
- "the stotra says grace is automatic. then what the hell do I do?"
- "if every rasa is the love/wonder/ānanda of consciousness transformed — grief is the
  love tasted in its absence — then is everything rooted in love?"
- "if this my body which is the level of the state of pain, an obstacle but not difficult.
  What is body? What is klesha dasha?"

The pattern:
1. **Start from the PRIOR session's conclusion.** Not from scratch — "the question that
   follows from X." The pushing is cumulative; each session assumes the last.
2. **One question.** Not a list of tensions. ONE thing you want to know.
3. **Colloquial.** "what the hell do I do?" not "what is the devotee's soteriological role?"
4. **The question is genuinely open.** Not a puzzle with a known answer — you're actually
   asking. The text might say something you didn't expect.
5. **Ground it in the text's OWN words first.** ROUND 0 is always "what the text actually
   says" — quoted, not paraphrased. The question emerges FROM the material, not before it.

## The process

### Step 1: Read the text until something bugs you
Don't start with a question. Read the 10 verses (or 49 passages) until one sentence
nags. The nagging IS the question. If you have to manufacture the tension, you're not
reading carefully enough.

### Step 2: State what the text says in its own words (ROUND 0)
Quote the Sanskrit. Quote Dyczkowski's lecture. Quote the argmap. Lay out the material
BEFORE asking anything. The material itself will reveal the tension.

### Step 3: Ask in your own voice, one question, starting from the prior conclusion
- ✗ "If X then how can Y?" (seminar prompt)
- ✓ "the stotra says grace is automatic. then what the hell do I do?" (someone confused)
- ✓ "is everything rooted in love? then what is FEAR?" (someone following the thread)
- ✗ "What is the relationship between A and B?" (definition request)
- ✓ "OK so the devotee just notices. That still sounds like doing nothing." (skeptical)

### Step 4: Penetrate (ROUND 1 → 2 → 3)
Each round: "OK that's the answer, but WHY?" or "OK that's the answer, but then what?"
- ROUND 1: the first answer from the text
- ROUND 2: the deeper "but why" — the text surprises you
- ROUND 3 (optional): the honest limit — where the text DOESN'T answer

### Step 5: End with honest limit, not a bow
The best sessions end with "the text doesn't address this" or "this is where it gets
hard." Not "therefore the doctrine is complete." The honest limit IS the gem sometimes.

## The smell test

Before shipping a pushing session, ask: **would I say this sentence out loud to someone
sitting next to me?** If no, rewrite it. Academic phrasing dies in the rewrite.

- ✗ "The stotra's structure performs the dialectical in devotional register"
- ✓ "the stotra doesn't just describe what the IPVV argues — it SINGS IT, over and over,
  until you can't forget it"

## Why this matters for the corpus

The pushing sessions are seed data for the embeddings loop (embeddings-loop.md: "the
probes are question-shaped seed data — what a human is likely to ask"). If the questions
are academic, the human's real questions won't match them. If the questions are in the
user's voice, the retrieval converges on actual curiosity.

Same logic applies to the chatbot: the pushing questions ARE the likely user queries.
Ask them like a user will ask them.
