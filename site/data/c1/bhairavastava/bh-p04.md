**SUMMARY.** Service to Śaṅkara and firm contemplation produce a state in which the devotee's
substance becomes terrifying Bhairava-power. The two practices are concurrent (not sequential)
and the result is a change of nature (-maya), not a grant of capability.

**FUNCTION.** Names the method: how transformation happens. Establishes sevanā + cintanā
as the operative practices.

**KEY TERMS.** sevanā (service) · cintanā (contemplation) · dhīra (firm/steady) ·
bhīṣaṇabhairavaśaktimaya (made of terrifying Bhairava-power) · -maya (suffix: composed of).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 4 of 10. After the concession (v3: suffering present), v4 names
the method that produces the state claimed in v5 (fearlessness) and v6 (bliss).

**EXPLANATION.** The two instrumentals — śaṅkarasevanā (by Śaṅkara-service) and
cintanādhīra (in contemplation-firm) — are grammatically concurrent: they describe the
devotee's condition, not a sequence. The result is bhīṣaṇabhairavaśaktimaya: the devotee
becomes -maya ("made of") power. This is not siddhi (acquired power) but svabhāva (nature
transformation) — the substance of the devotee changes, not the devotee's inventory of
abilities.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Not claimed: that the devotee gains Bhairava's powers or becomes
Bhairava. The -maya suffix claims composition ("made of power"), not identity ("is
Bhairava"). The causal structure (does sevanā produce cintanā, or do both independently
produce the result?) is grammatically ambiguous.

**RELATED.** v1 (the achieved-state reading of tvanmaya), v6 (bliss through fullness),
Lakshmanjoo's teaching on bhairava-śakti as nature not attribute.
