**SUMMARY.** BLOCKED — no L1/L2. The Sanskrit claims constitutive identity: the devotee's
self is "made of" Bhairava's self (svātmamaya), always (sadaiva), with bidirectional
possession (whatever is Bhairava's is the devotee's, and vice versa).

**FUNCTION.** The stotra's ontological climax — the identity claim that the devotional
posture (v1) and the results (v5–v8) all presuppose.

**KEY TERMS.** sadaiva (always) · mamā'tmā (my own-self) · svātmamaya (made of Your
own-self) · samastam (all) · maheśa (the Great Lord).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 2 of 10. Follows the surrender (v1) and precedes the concession
(suffering, v3). The identity claim is the pivot: surrender (v1) → identity (v2) →
suffering acknowledged (v3) → method (v4).

**EXPLANATION.** BLOCKED — requires L1 before English can be trusted. The Sanskrit claim
is: the self is constitutively Bhairava's (svātmamaya), and this is perennial (sadaiva).
This is not philosophical identity (aham śiva) but devotional constitutiveness — the
grammar preserves subject-object even while claiming composition. The bidirectional
possession (mama tena samastam) means the relationship is not one-directional devotion
but mutual indwelling.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** BLOCKED — needs L1 for: (1) whether samastam means "all possessions"
or "all reality"; (2) whether sadaiva is metaphysical (always the case) or epistemic
(recognition is always available).

**RELATED.** v1 (surrender), v7 (nondifference — the conditional version of this claim),
IPK 1.1 (the same identity grammar in Utpaladeva).
