**SUMMARY.** Salutation to the Lord removes all fear of death. The devotee names four
mortal threats — death, Yama, the ender, karma-ghosts — and claims categorical
fearlessness (na jātu bibhemi, "never at all do I fear") through namas alone.

**FUNCTION.** The efficacy claim: devotion (namas) works against the ultimate threat
(mortality). Makes explicit what v1 (surrender) and v3 (non-flinching) imply.

**KEY TERMS.** namas (salutation/submission) · mṛtyu (death) · Yama (lord of death) ·
antaka (the ender) · karmapiśāca (karma-ghosts) · na jātu bibhemi (never at all I fear).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 5 of 10. The consequence of v4 (method → power): power manifests
as fearlessness against the full spectrum of death.

**EXPLANATION.** The four threats are not decorative — they cover mortality exhaustively:
mṛtyu (the event), Yama (the administrator), antaka (the agent), karmapiśāca (the karmic
residue). The verb's absolute force (na jātu = "never at all") makes this categorical, not
probabilistic. Critically, the verse claims fearlessness (na bibhemi), not immortality
(na mriye). The distinction matters: the devotee will still die, but will not fear dying.
This is the Trika soteriology of jīvanmukta applied to its most concrete case.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Not claimed: that the devotee survives death, or that namas produces
liberation (mokṣa). The four threats may be four names for one thing (death's aspects) or
four distinct entities — the verse lists them as separate accusatives, but tradition
often collapses them. Whether "never at all" is current-state or permanent is unresolved.

**RELATED.** v3 (suffering acknowledged — the concession that makes fearlessness meaningful),
v10 (cosmic heat pacified — the same mortality, universalized), Tantrāloka 1 (jīvanmukti).
