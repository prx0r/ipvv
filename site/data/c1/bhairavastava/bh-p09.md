**SUMMARY.** BLOCKED — no L1/L2. The Sanskrit attributes four qualities to Bhairava:
dear (priyam), beautiful (sudarśanam), unique (ekam), and hard-to-reach by others
(durlabham anyajanaiḥ) — plus covenant-knowing (samayajñam). The devotee has access
others lack, through the samaya (tantric covenant), not through merit.

**FUNCTION.** Praise-verse that also makes a relational claim: the devotee's access is
covenantal, not earned. Bridges the identity claim (v2) and the cosmic close (v10).

**KEY TERMS.** samaya (covenant/pact) · durlabham (hard to obtain) · sudarśanam
(beautiful to behold) · ekam (unique) · samayajñam (knowing the covenant).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 9 of 10. The penultimate verse: after the personal arc
(v1–v8), it names why this devotee specifically has access — through samaya — before
the cosmic close (v10).

**EXPLANATION.** BLOCKED — requires L1. The Sanskrit attributes priya (dear), sudarśana
(beautiful), eka (unique), durlabha (rare/hard-to-reach) — a set of praises. The key
term samayajñam ("knowing the covenant") makes this relational: Bhairava knows the
samaya (the tantric pact), and the devotee's access is through that pact, not through
merit or method. This distinguishes the stotra's soteriology from karmic or jñānic
frameworks: access is covenantal.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** BLOCKED — needs L1 for: (1) whether samayajñam means "covenant-
knowing" (knowledge of agreement) or "time-knowing" (kāla-samaya, knowing the appointed
time); (2) whether "hard to obtain by others" is factual or devotional intensification.

**RELATED.** v2 (identity — the covenant's basis), v10 (the cosmic result of covenantal
access), the stotra's arc from personal (v1) to cosmic (v10).
