**SUMMARY.** The compassionate Lord instantly pacifies the world's great burning heat.
This closes the stotra's arc from personal surrender (v1) to cosmic compassion (v10):
the Lord's nature (dayā, compassion) produces the effect (śamana, pacification) without
method or delay (jhaṭiti, in a flash).

**FUNCTION.** Cosmic close. Universalizes the stotra's personal claims: what was individual
(fearlessness, bliss, nondifference) becomes world-pacification.

**KEY TERMS.** arusantāpa (great burning heat of the world) · śamayati (pacifies/soothes) ·
jhaṭiti (in a flash) · dayāluḥ (compassionate) · vibhuḥ (the Lord as all-pervading).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 10 of 10. The stotra's final movement: from "I bow" (v1, personal)
to "the world's heat is pacified" (cosmic). Each verse has expanded the scope: personal
surrender → personal fearlessness → personal bliss → personal nondifference → cosmic
compassion.

**EXPLANATION.** The causal agent is the Lord's nature (dayā, compassion), not the devotee's
effort — this is the stotra's closing theology: pacification happens by compassion, not by
method. The adverb jhaṭiti ("in a flash") is the most radical temporal claim in the text
— instantaneous cosmic pacification. The tension with v3 (suffering acknowledged as present)
is deliberate and unresolved: the heat is always present AND always being pacified. This
is the perennial-present soteriology: the Lord is always cooling, the devotee is always
being relieved — and the recognition of both is the stotra's own claim.

**Dyczkowski lecture.** Dyczkowski dates the colophon: Vasura-saṃvatsara, Pauṣa, kṛṣṇadaśamī. Padyanavaka = nine verses (colophon counts 9, the opening vyāpta-verse being invocation). Tradition: cave entry with 10,000 disciples, chanting this hymn, then disappearance.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Not claimed: eschatological final pacification (the world will end),
nor that suffering is already gone (v3 contradicts). The present tense (śamayati) and
jhaṭiti suggest a standing offer, not a future event. Whether "world's heat" (arusaṃtāpa)
is saṃsāra, delusion, or karmic consequence is not specified.

**RELATED.** v1 (the personal stance that the cosmic close resolves), v3 (the suffering
that the pacification answers), Tantrāloka's teaching on compassion as śakti's movement.
