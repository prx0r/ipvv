**SUMMARY.** The devotee acknowledges suffering, delusion, and terror-causing karma as
present — and does not flinch. Suffering is not denied; it is held within the devotional
relationship established in v1.

**FUNCTION.** Prevents the stotra from reading as wish-fulfillment. Grounds devotion in
realism about pain.

**KEY TERMS.** duḥkha (suffering) · moha (delusion) · karmapiśāca (karma-ghosts) ·
satsvapi (even though present).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Verse 3 of 10. Follows v1 (surrender) and v2 (identity, BLOCKED).
Establishes the concession that makes v5 (fearlessness) meaningful — you cannot be fearless
about something you don't acknowledge.

**EXPLANATION.** The particle satsvapi ("even though present") does the whole work: it
concedes the reality of the conditions (duḥkha, moha, bhaya) without qualifying them.
The devotee's non-flinching is implied, not stated — the verse lists what is present and
stops. This is the stotra's epistemology: suffering is real, and the devotee's stance
toward it is unflinching rather than denying. It is not māyā-philosophy (suffering is
illusion) but śūnyatā-adjacent realism (suffering is present, and the response is not
aversion).

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** Not claimed: that suffering ends, that it is illusory, or that the
devotee is insensitive to it. The three conditions (duḥkha, moha, karmapiśāca) are listed
without causal connectives — whether they form a sequence or a simultaneous set is
unresolved.

**RELATED.** v5 (fearlessness — the consequence of non-flinching), v10 (cosmic heat
pacified — the same conditions, universalized).
