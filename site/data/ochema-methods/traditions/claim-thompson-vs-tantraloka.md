# CLAIM: The Vedic cosmos (Bhū-maṇḍala, 14 lokas) and the Śaiva cosmos (brahmāṇḍa, seven lokas, 36 tattvas) are two systematic orderings of one inhabited universe.

STATUS: C (correspondence) — two systems addressing the same structural problem; never upgraded to T or E by accumulation.
LABEL: interpretive (a mapping between traditions, not a verified text claim)

SOURCE:
  Text: Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Canto 5 + Thompson, Vedic Cosmography; Tantrāloka Āhnika 8–10 (per ro-tantraloka-cosmology)
  Translation: Dyczkowski (TĀ); Thompson/Prabhupāda (SB)

WHAT IT SAYS:
  Both traditions order the cosmos as a *hierarchy of inhabited worlds*
  along a vertical axis, centered on Mount Meru/Sumeru, with higher,
  middle, and lower realms each populated by specific beings.

MAPPING (the structural correspondence):

| Dimension | Bhagavata (Thompson) | Tantrāloka (Abhinavagupta) | Status |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------------|--------|
| Cosmic egg | brahmāṇḍa, egg of Brahmā, ~4-billion-mile disc (Bhū-maṇḍala) | brahmāṇḍa, Egg of Brahmā, measured in yojanas (TĀ 8) | C — shared frame |
| Central axis | Mount Sumeru (gold), Brahmapurī on summit | Mount Meru, Jambūdvīpa at center | C |
| Upper worlds | Svarga, Maharloka, Janaloka, Tapoloka, Satyaloka (SB 5.24) | Bhūr, Bhuvar, Svar, Mahar, Jana, Tapas, Satya (TĀ 8) | C — near-identical list |
| Middle/earth | Bhū-maṇḍala, 7 dvīpas, Jambūdvīpa | Jambūdvīpa + Bhārata-varṣa (TĀ 8) | C |
| Lower worlds | 7 subterranean heavens (bila-svarga): Atala→Pātāla (SB 5.24) | 8 netherworlds (pātāla) + 35 hells (TĀ 8) | C — both enumerate the same structure |
| Beings | Demigods, Siddhas, Gandharvas, Nāgas, Dānavas, Yakṣas | The 7 perceivers (sakala→śiva), the powers/śaktis | C — different ontology of the inhabitants |
| Who you are | A conditioned soul in a graded realm, per karma | A contracted perceiver (pramātṛ), per karma/mala | C — shared karmic frame |

THE KEY DIVERGENCE (where they are NOT the same system):

- **Number & kind of worlds.** Bhagavata = a spatial/geographical universe
  (14 lokas, 7 dvīpas, many planets). Tantrāloka = a *metaphysical* universe:
  the 36 tattvas are *principles* (consciousness, powers, elements), and the
  "207 worlds" (Bhuvanādhvan) are levels of the subtle body and initiation
  paths (the ṣaḍadhvan), not merely places in space.
- **What the axis is.** For Thompson/SB, Sumeru is a physical-gold mountain
  that is higher-dimensional. For Abhinavagupta, the ascent is through the
  *tattvas* — a movement of consciousness through the planes of reality, not
  a spatial travel. Meru in TĀ is more symbol-schematic than topographical.
- **The role of the subtle body.** TĀ 8 gives the puryaṣṭaka (subtle body,
  'City of Eight') that transmigrates — the worlds are in some sense *inside*
  the perceiver's constitution. Thompson keeps the worlds as externally real
  higher-dimensional places. (TĀ's interiority is the deeper claim.)

ROOM FOR RESEARCH:
  Yes. The correspondence is genuine (both: hierarchy of inhabited worlds,
  karma-driven rebirth, liberation as ascent) but they diverge on whether the
  worlds are *external geography* (SB/Thompson) or *levels of the self's own
  constitution* (TĀ's tattva-ontology). This is a real philosophical fork, not
  a conflation. The most productive line: does Abhinavagupta's puryaṣṭaka
  provide the *subjective correlate* of Thompson's 'dimensionality as access'?

# CLAIM: Thompson's "dimensionality as degree of access" and Abhinavagupta's "kañcukas as contraction of capacity" address the same structural problem: why the one consciousness appears as many finite perceivers/worlds.

STATUS: C (correspondence)
LABEL: interpretive

SOURCE:
  Text: Thompson, chs. 2–3 (dimensionality-as-access); TĀ 9 (the five kañcukas: kalā, vidyā, rāga, kāla, niyati)
  Translation: Dyczkowski (TĀ)

WHAT IT SAYS:
  Thompson: beings perceive different 'dimensionalities' (degrees of access
  to other places) because of their sensory constitution.
  Abhinavagupta: the one Śiva contracts his infinite capacities into finite
  perception via the five kañcukas (limited agency, limited knowledge,
  attachment, time, necessity).

MAPPING:
  Both are theories of *how the same reality appears limited*:
  - Thompson: limitation = restricted access (a sensory/dimensional fact)
  - TĀ: limitation = contraction of capacity (kañcukas) + māyā
  The kañcukas are, in effect, the *mechanism* of Thompson's 'restricted
  sensory constitution.' Kāla (time) and niyati (necessity) explain why
  access is bounded; the other three explain bounded agency/knowledge/desire.

ROOM FOR RESEARCH:
  Yes — this is the strongest productive correspondence. Thompson's
  'access' is a geometric/sensory metaphor; Abhinavagupta gives the
  metaphysical *cause* (the kañcukas). A unified framework could read
  Thompson's higher-dimensionality as the *appearance* produced by
  Abhinavagupta's contraction. But per the inheritance rule: the Śaiva
  cause does not inherit empirical status from Thompson's geometric model,
  and vice-versa. Both stay C.

# CLAIM: Thompson's inhabited lower worlds (bila-svarga) and Abhinavagupta's pātāla/netherworlds describe the same lower realms.

STATUS: C (correspondence) — near-identical geography, different metaphysics.
LABEL: interpretive

SOURCE:
  Text: SB 5.24.7–16 (Atala→Pātāla); TĀ 8 (the 8 pātāla + 35 hells)
  Translation: Prabhupāda; Dyczkowski

WHAT IT SAYS:
  Both enumerate a set of lower worlds below the earth. But:
  - SB: bila-svarga = opulent 'imitation heavens' where Daityas/Dānavas/Nāgas
    enjoy sense pleasure (free of ageing/disease, but bound by time).
  - TĀ: the pātāla are described more briefly; the accent is on the *hells*
    (Avīci, Kumbhīpāka, Mahāraurava) as consequences of karma.

ROOM FOR RESEARCH:
  Moderate. The correspondence shows the shared cosmological grammar (seven
  lower worlds, serpents/Nāgas, subterranean location). The divergence — SB's
  opulent-but-illusory bila-svarga vs TĀ's karmic hells — is a genuine
  theological difference worth noting, not an error.

---
# CONCLUSION (the honest verdict)

Bhagavata/Thompson and Tantrāloka share a **cosmological grammar**: a
karma-governed, hierarchically inhabited universe centered on Meru, with
seven upper and seven lower worlds. The correspondence is real and rich.

They diverge on the **nature of the worlds**:
- Bhagavata/Thompson: the worlds are *external, spatially-real,
  higher-dimensional places* populated by beings.
- Tantrāloka: the worlds are *levels of consciousness* (tattvas,
  ṣaḍadhvan) and the subtle body — the cosmos is largely *interior*.

Per the ochema METHOD, all comparisons are **C (correspondence)** and
**interpretive** — none inherit empirical or textual status by accumulation.
Thompson's 'higher-dimensional access' is best read as the *appearance* of
Abhinavagupta's kañcuka-contraction, but this is a hypothesis, not a proof.
