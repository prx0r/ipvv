SPANDA: THE PULSE
A PULSE-FIRST WORKBOOK
What is the vibration that is already everything?
Why does the world seem still and separate when it is a throb?
Why does the pulse need the body, the breath, and the senses?

PURPOSE
This workbook gives the Spandakārikā a logical entry point. It does not ask you to accept
Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics. It reconstructs the questions the system asks, the answers it
proposes, why each concept is introduced, and where the contested inferences remain.
Prepared as part of the Tantrāloka × Ochema research programme · August 2026

---
HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK
Do not read the Spandakārikā as verses to memorize. Read it as one escalating problem:
*if reality is a single pulsing consciousness, why does it present itself as a still world
of separate things, observed by a separate "me"?*
1. Read one unit at a time. The units are cumulative.
2. Keep three columns: what the text claims / what Ochema translates / what remains unproven.
3. Do the checkpoints. Conceptual control, not memorization.
4. Treat every modern comparison as an analogue, never an identity.
5. Return to the kārikā map only after Units 1–5.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE
Never allow a mathematical theorem, neuroscientific model, or contemplative experience to
inherit the truth of a metaphysical claim merely because their structures resemble one
another. Translation, analogy, implementation, and identity are four different relations.

THE ONE PROBLEM
A reality that merely *shines* would be a lamp, not awareness. To be consciousness, it must
*throb* — and that throb is both the world's manifestation and its self-knowing.

UNIT 1 — WHAT IS THE PULSE?
The tradition's own term-policy is decisive: spanda = "the pulse," never "vibration,"
which risks sounding mechanical. It is the self-moving activity of consciousness itself.

1.1 The source of the wheel of energies
The root verse names the absolute as the pulse's source:

> taṃ śakticakravibhavaprabhavaṃ śaṃkaraṃ stumaḥ —
> "we praise Śaṅkara, the source of the Wheel of Energies."
> — Spandakārikā 1.1 (the dossier's anchor rendering)

1.2 Not a thing that vibrates
Spanda is not a substance that shakes. It is the very life of awareness — the movement by
which consciousness knows and manifests at once. It is constitutive, not accidental.

1.3 The hidden pulse driving the triad
> yataḥ kāraṇavargo 'yaṃ vimūḍho 'mūḍhavat svayam | sahāntareṇa cakreṇa
> pravṛttisthitisaṃhṛtīḥ —
> "the host of causes, bewildered-as-if-not, by the inner wheel achieves creation,
> maintenance, dissolution."
> — Spandakārikā 1.6 (the hidden pulse driving the triad — the report's A-thesis in the
> Spanda's form)

The world's apparent stillness is the hidden pulse working unnoticed.

CHECKPOINT 1
• Why "pulse" and not "vibration"?
• What does kārikā 1.6 reveal about the relation of the pulse to the visible world?

UNIT 2 — WHY DOES IT FEEL STILL?
If reality is a single throb, why does experience present a still world of separate,
solid things?

2.1 The perceiver's contraction
The world does not appear pulsing because the perceiver is contracted. The individual soul,
absorbed in its own standpoint, loses the felt sense of the pulse. It is not that the pulse
stops; it is that the perceiver stops being open to it.

2.2 Not an illusion of the world, but a limit of the perceiver
Spanda does not say the world is unreal. It says the world's pulsing nature is overlooked
by a contracted view. The stillness is a misperception of a throb, not proof that reality
is still.

2.3 The caution
That experience has a temporal flow is a datum. That this flow is the absolute's own
cognition-sequence is the metaphysical claim.

CHECKPOINT 2
• When the world seems still, what has changed — the world or the perceiver?
• Why is "spanda = energy" a category error under the methodological rule?

UNIT 3 — WHAT IS CONSCIOUSNESS, IF IT PULSES?

3.1 Manifestation is self-showing (prakāśa)
Consciousness appears. Nothing is known without it.

3.2 Self-knowing is the pulse's return (vimarśa)
Consciousness knows its own appearing. The pulse is the name for the inseparability of the
two: to manifest IS to know oneself manifesting.

3.3 The perceiver never deviates
The subject is not destroyed in liberation; it is recognized as having always been the
pulse. The apparent "fall" into a bounded self was a misrecognition, not a departure of
reality from itself.

3.4 The integrated picture
Consciousness = activity that knows itself = spanda. Every cognition is a pulse: the
reaching out toward an object and the taking it back into the self.

CHECKPOINT 3
• What is vimarśa, and why is it the pulse's "return"?
• How can cognition and manifestation be the same movement?

UNIT 4 — HOW DOES THE WORLD ARISE?

4.1 The wheel of powers (śakticakra)
Every faculty — perception, memory, imagination, agency — is a spoke of the wheel whose
source is Śiva. The many powers are not separate things; they are the one consciousness
moving through its self-expressions.

4.2 The dissolution of the subject/object split
There is not a "me" and a "world" with powers in between. There is one wheel, one source,
and its spokes are the apparent actors and objects. The finite self is a spoke that has
forgotten it belongs to the wheel.

4.3 World as self-articulation
The world is the shape the pulse takes in its self-knowing — the dynamism-pole of
manifestation, complementing the recognition-pole of Pratyabhijñā.

CHECKPOINT 4
• What is the śakticakra, and what is its source?
• How does the wheel dissolve the subject/object split?

UNIT 5 — HOW DOES THE BODY AND BREATH FIT?
The pulse is not only metaphysical; it is felt as the body's life and breath.

5.1 The pulse in the body
Spanda is the very life of the body — the breath, the heartbeat, the flow of sensation.
The body is not a cage around the pulse; it is one of the pulse's own articulations.

5.2 Breath and sensation as access
By attending to the body's own pulsing, the yogi recovers the felt sense of spanda that
ordinary perception overlooks. The body is a doorway to the pulse, not a barrier.

5.3 The caution
That the body "feels alive" is a datum. That this aliveness is the absolute's own
self-movement is the metaphysical claim.

CHECKPOINT 5
• Why is the body a doorway, not a barrier?
• What practice-gate does the body offer, and what must not be inferred from it?

UNIT 6 — WHAT CHANGES IN LIBERATION?

6.1 Before and after
Before recognition, the perceiver feels bounded, separate, still. After, it recognizes
itself as the pulse. Nothing is added; a mistaken identification is corrected.

6.2 Liberation as recognition, not escape
Spanda aligns with Pratyabhijñā: liberation is not leaving the world but recognizing the
ever-present dynamic self-nature. The wheel never left its hub.

6.3 The lived difference
Though nothing is added, everything is felt differently — the world experienced as the
pulse's own body.

CHECKPOINT 6
• Why can't liberation be the acquisition of a new state?
• How does Spanda's liberation relate to recognition?

UNIT 7 — WHY THE FOUR COMMENTARIES?

7.1 One verse, four depths
The Spandakārikā survives with four commentaries (Vṛtti, Vivṛti, Nirṇaya, Saṃdoha),
edited together by Dyczkowski. The same stanza is read at four levels: the gloss, the
doctrinal unfolding, the analytical determination, and the concentrated summary.

7.2 The reading method
A beginner reads the gloss; a scholar the analysis; a practitioner the summary as a
meditation. This is the workbook's three-depth display applied to the primary text itself.

7.3 What remains contested
How far the pulse is an actual felt process vs a structural description; the relation of
Spanda to the Śivasūtra's authorship; the divergences of the four commentaries.

UNIT 8 — WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

8.1 The strongest Spanda chain
Manifestation is self-showing; self-showing is self-knowing; self-knowing is the pulse; the
pulse is the absolute.

8.2 The contested jump
That the felt aliveness of the body is the absolute's own self-movement is the metaphysical
leap — the tradition's claim, not an established fact.

8.3 What Ochema can contribute
Map the pulse onto the dynamism pole; compare with modern self-organization and active
inference — as correspondence, never identity.

8.4 What Ochema must not claim yet
That physics "proves" spanda, or that spanda "proves" a physics.

8.5 Final synthesis
Spanda gives the system its energy: the pulse that both manifests and knows. It connects
the precision of Pratyabhijñā (recognition) to the process-ontology of Krama (sequence).
The Krama's own signature captures it in one image:

> spandāspandaparispandamakarandamahotpalam —
> "the great lotus of the nectar that is the pulse, the pulse's rest, the pulse's
> outpouring."
> — Mahārthamañjarī 2 (the Krama reading of the pulse-triple)
