KRAMA: THE SEQUENCE OF COGNITION
A SEQUENCE-FIRST WORKBOOK
What if consciousness is not a single static witness but an ordered sequence?
Why does the order of worship reflect the order of knowing?
Why are the Kālīs moments of cognition, not just goddesses?

PURPOSE
This workbook gives the Krama tradition a logical entry point. It does not ask you to
accept Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics. It reconstructs the questions the system asks, the
answers it proposes, why each concept is introduced, and where the contested inferences
remain.
Prepared as part of the Tantrāloka × Ochema research programme · August 2026

---
HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK
Do not read the Krama as goddesses and rituals to memorize. Read it as one escalating
problem: *if reality is consciousness, and consciousness moves, then what is the order of
that movement — and how do we take part in it?*
1. Read one unit at a time. The units are cumulative.
2. Keep three columns: what the text claims / what Ochema translates / what remains unproven.
3. Do the checkpoints. Conceptual control, not memorization.
4. Treat every modern comparison as an analogue, never an identity.
5. Return to the text map only after Units 1–6.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE
Never allow a mathematical theorem, neuroscientific model, or contemplative experience to
inherit the truth of a metaphysical claim merely because their structures resemble one
another. Translation, analogy, implementation, and identity are four different relations.

THE ONE PROBLEM
A consciousness that merely *was* would be a static eye. To act, it must move — and to
move, it must have an order. The Krama asks: what is the order of consciousness's own
self-activity, and how do we enter it?

UNIT 1 — WHAT IS SEQUENCE?
The name of the tradition is its thesis. Krama = "order" / "sequence." But the Krama does
not say that things are ordered; it says that order IS cognition.

1.1 Sequence is not trivial
The sequence in which consciousness moves is not incidental to what consciousness is. To
know is to move through an order. The order is not a container for cognition; it is
cognition's very form.

1.2 The escalating problem
If reality is a single consciousness, it cannot be a frozen eye. It must act. But action
implies a before and after. The Krama asks what that order is, and how a finite being takes
part in it.

1.3 The sharp distinction
That experience has a temporal flow is a datum. That this flow is the absolute's own
cognition-sequence is the metaphysical claim.

UNIT 2 — WHY WORSHIP IN ORDER?

2.1 The pūjā-krama
The perceptible order of worship is the pūjā-krama. The Krama holds that this order
corresponds to — and enacts — the imperceptible sequence of cognition (saṃvit-krama).

2.2 Ritual as the grammar of knowing
Ritual is not a performance laid over a static reality. It is the visible grammar of how
consciousness works: how it emerges, expands, rests, dissolves. The worshipper does not
perform an order; the worshipper *enters* the order by which consciousness knows itself.

2.3 The caution
That ritual has a structure is a datum. That this structure is consciousness's own
cognition-sequence is the metaphysical claim.

CHECKPOINT 2
• What is the pūjā-krama, and what does it correspond to?
• How is ritual "the grammar of knowing" rather than a performance?

UNIT 3 — WHO ARE THE KĀLĪS?
The most esoteric teaching of the tradition is the Doctrine of the Twelve Kālīs. Wenta
opens her study with its importance:

> "The Doctrine of the Twelve Kālīs constitutes one of the earliest developments of the
> Kālīkula/Kālīkrama. Moreover, it represents the most esoteric teaching in the entire
> Kashmiri Śaiva tradition predominant in the Kashmir region in the early medieval period."
> — Aleksandra Wenta, *The Twelve Kālīs and Utpaladeva's Appraisal of the Sensory
> Experience*

3.1 Kālīs are moments, not merely goddesses
The Twelve Kālīs are the twelve moments of cognition — the phases through which
consciousness moves as it knows. The goddess is the personified form of the cognitive
moment.

3.2 Kālasaṁkarṣaṇī, the crown
> "She Who Draws Time to Herself (Kālasaṁkarṣaṇī), the goddess of the Kālīkrama, is Beyond
> Parā (Parātīta) of the Trika goddesses... that underline the doctrinal foundation of his
> Trika system."
> — Wenta, *The Twelve Kālīs*

The Krama crowns the Trika's triad with a goddess of the very principle of sequence.

3.3 The deep link to Pratyabhijñā
Wenta traces the lineage: the Krama teacher Govindarāja → Somānanda → Utpaladeva →
Abhinavagupta. The Doctrine of the Twelve Kālīs passed down this very chain. Sequence and
recognition are two halves of one insight.

CHECKPOINT 3
• What are the Twelve Kālīs, and why are they "moments"?
• Who is Kālasaṁkarṣaṇī, and why is she "beyond Parā"?

UNIT 4 — HOW DOES SEQUENCE RELATE TO RECOGNITION?

4.1 Process and recognition complement
If reality is a sequence of cognitive moments, liberation is recognizing oneself as the
*whole* sequence — as the Kālīs' return to Kālasaṁkarṣaṇī — rather than as a single moment
mistaken for a separate self.

4.2 One insight, two expressions
Pratyabhijñā says "you are the one consciousness that knows." The Krama says "you are the
ordered sequence by which it knows." Both dissolve the separate self; they differ in
whether they accent the static identity or the dynamic order.

4.3 Wenta's fusion point
Wenta locates where the Krama and Pratyabhijñā fuse — in the sensory, in devotion:

> "Indulgence in the senses entails entering into the highest realization of Śiva's
> nature, it is a sudden recognition (sāhasa-abhijñāna)... 'saturated with the pure,
> everlastingly flowing stream of the nectar of consciousness (saṁvit-amṛta)'... that
> comprises the cognition 'that all is one.'"
> — Wenta, *The Twelve Kālīs*

The sequence of sense-experience, followed to its end, is a recognition.

CHECKPOINT 4
• How is liberation-as-recognition compatible with liberation-as-sequence?
• Where, according to Wenta, do the Krama and Pratyabhijñā fuse?

UNIT 5 — WHAT IS THE MAHĀNĀYA?

5.1 Reality as the ever-complete sequence
The Mahānaya ("Great Method") and Mahārtha ("Great Meaning"), in the Mahānayaprakāśa and
Mahārthamañjarī, read the whole of reality as the ever-present, already-complete
cognition-sequence of the Goddess.

5.2 Not a ladder to climb
The Mahānaya is a recognition of what is already the case: the sequence of the world is the
Goddess's own, complete from the first moment. The Kālīs do not have to be assembled; they
are already one movement.

5.3 The process-based recognition
This is the process-based equivalent of the recognition thesis: liberation as the
re-cognition of an ever-present whole.

CHECKPOINT 5
• What is the Mahānaya, and why is it "already complete"?
• How is the Mahānaya a process-based version of recognition?

UNIT 6 — HOW IS LIBERATION PROCESS-BASED?

6.1 Sequence returns to itself
The sequence does not end; it returns. Consciousness emerges, expands, rests, dissolves —
and this very movement, recognized as the whole, is liberation. There is no static "beyond
the sequence"; there is the sequence seen as one complete movement.

6.2 The lived difference
Before recognition, one is a single moment caught in the flow. After, one is the whole
sequence — not still, but complete in motion.

CHECKPOINT 6
• Why can't liberation be a static escape?
• What is the difference between "a moment in the flow" and "the whole sequence"?

UNIT 7 — WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

7.1 The strongest Krama chain
Consciousness moves; movement is ordered; the order is cognition; the Kālīs are its
moments; recognition is the whole.

7.2 The contested points
The exact number and structure of the Kālī-moments (Twelve vs the Ten of the daśākhaṇḍa);
how far the ritual enacted the cognition; the relation of the Mahānaya to the earlier
Kālīkula texts.

7.3 What Ochema can contribute
Map the cognition-sequence onto the process/dynamism pole; compare with sequential/process
models of cognition — as correspondence, never identity.

7.4 What Ochema must not claim yet
That cognitive science "proves" the Kālī-moments, or that the Kālīs "prove" a cognitive
model.

7.5 Final synthesis
Krama supplies the process-based reading: reality as the ordered sequence of cognition. It
balances the static precision of Pratyabhijñā and connects the Kālī-moments to the Goddess
who is their whole.
