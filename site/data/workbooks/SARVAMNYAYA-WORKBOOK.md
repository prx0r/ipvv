SARVĀMNĀYA: THE SYNTHESIS
A CROSSWALK-FIRST WORKBOOK
What happens when incompatible traditions are deliberately combined?
How can Kubjikā, Kālī, Tripurā, and Siddhalakṣmī become one system?
What does this reveal about the nature of the traditions themselves?

PURPOSE
This workbook gives the Sarvāmnāya / Newar (Nepalese multi-āmnāya) synthesis a logical
entry point. It does not ask you to accept Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics. It reconstructs the
questions the system asks, the answers it proposes, why each concept is introduced, and
where the contested inferences remain.
Prepared as part of the Tantrāloka × Ochema research programme · August 2026

HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK
Do not read the Sarvāmnāya as combined rituals to memorize. Read it as one escalating
problem: *if each tradition is founded on its own revelation, goddess, and initiation, how
could they ever be combined — and what does the combining reveal?*
1. Read one unit at a time. The units are cumulative.
2. Keep three columns: what the text claims / what Ochema translates / what remains unproven.
3. Do the checkpoints. Conceptual control, not memorization.
4. Treat every modern comparison as an analogue, never an identity.
5. Return to the text map only after Units 1–6.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE
Never allow a mathematical theorem, neuroscientific model, or contemplative experience to
inherit the truth of a metaphysical claim merely because their structures resemble one
another. Translation, analogy, implementation, and identity are four different relations.

THE ONE PROBLEM
If the traditions each claim their own goddess, text, and path, how could they be combined
without destroying what makes each distinctive? The Sarvāmnāya is the tradition's own
answer.

UNIT 1 — WHAT IS A "FULL TRANSMISSION"?

1.1 The name
Sarvāmnāya = "all the transmissions" (sarva + āmnāya). It names the Nepalese multi-āmnāya
syntheses: traditions that deliberately combine Kubjikā, Kālī, Tripurā, Siddhalakṣmī, and
others into integrated systems.

1.2 Not a single tradition
The Sarvāmnāya is not one more school. It is a synthesis that deliberately holds several
traditions together in one system — the endgame of the corpus, where incompatible systems
were made mutually translatable by practitioners themselves.

1.3 The escalating problem
If each tradition is founded on its own revelation, its own goddess, and its own
initiation, then how can they be combined without destroying what makes each distinctive?

CHECKPOINT 1
• Why is Sarvāmnāya "all the transmissions" rather than one more school?
• What problem does deliberate combination pose for the distinct traditions?

UNIT 2 — WHY COMBINE?

2.1 Mutual translatability
The atlas notes that the synthesis arose because practitioners themselves made the
incompatible systems mutually translatable. The combining was not a scholar's abstraction;
it was a lived practice — deities, texts, and rites brought into relation so that one
initiation could open several traditions.

2.2 The motive
The motive was not syncretism for its own sake. It was the recognition that the different
traditions could be held together — their goddesses and rites arranged into one working
system.

2.3 The caution
That traditions were combined is a historical datum. What the combination means
theologically is the tradition's claim.

CHECKPOINT 2
• What does "mutually translatable" mean as a lived practice?
• Why is this more than mere syncretism?

UNIT 3 — HOW IS THIS DONE?

3.1 A crosswalk of goddesses and rites
The synthesis works by a crosswalk: the goddesses of the different āmnāyas (Kubjikā, Kālī,
Tripurā, Siddhalakṣmī) are arranged into a single hierarchy, and their rites into a single
ritual calendar. The distinct traditions are not erased; they are related.

3.2 The integrated ritual system
The result is a multi-āmnāya ritual system in which a practitioner can move across the
traditions. This is the "crosswalk layer": the mechanism by which distinct systems are
held together.

CHECKPOINT 3
• What is the crosswalk, and how does it relate the goddesses?
• Why are the traditions "related" rather than "erased"?

UNIT 4 — WHAT DOES THIS TEST?

4.1 The semantic-model test
For the ochema project, the Sarvāmnāya is a test case: can the semantic model recognize
several traditions being deliberately combined? If the model can only handle traditions
one at a time, it cannot describe the synthesis.

4.2 The crosswalk layer
The crosswalk layer tracks how distinct traditions are related — their shared structures,
their deliberate unions. The Sarvāmnāya is the strongest challenge to that layer.

CHECKPOINT 4
• Why is the Sarvāmnāya a test for the semantic model?
• What is the "crosswalk layer" and why does the synthesis stress it?

UNIT 5 — WHY NEPAL?

5.1 Where the manuscripts survive
The multi-āmnāya synthesis is preserved overwhelmingly in Nepal, where the Kubjikā, Kālī,
and other manuscripts survived and where Newar ritual continuity persisted.

5.2 Ritual continuity
Nepal preserves not only the texts but the living ritual tradition — the Newar paddhatis
(ritual handbooks) that encode the synthesis in practice.

5.3 The caution
That Nepal preserved the material is a historical fact. That the synthesis is the corpus's
culmination is the interpretation.

CHECKPOINT 5
• Why is Nepal decisive for the Sarvāmnāya?
• What do the Newar paddhatis preserve beyond the texts?

UNIT 6 — WHAT IS THE ENDGAME?

6.1 The corpus's culmination
The Sarvāmnāya is the endgame of the corpus — the point where the distinct traditions,
having each been studied, are seen to be combinable. It tests the whole model.

6.2 What it reveals
The synthesis reveals that the traditions are not isolated monads; they share enough
structure to be deliberately united. What the other workbooks study one at a time (the
Trika's recognition, the Krama's sequence, the Kaula's kula, the Kubjikā's phoneme), the
Sarvāmnāya holds together.

CHECKPOINT 6
• Why is the Sarvāmnāya the "endgame"?
• What does the possibility of synthesis reveal about the traditions?

UNIT 7 — WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

7.1 The provisional chain
Several āmnāyas exist; practitioners combined them; the combination is a crosswalk; Nepal
preserved it; the synthesis tests the semantic model. The chain is coherent in outline,
but the primary-source documentation is thinner than for the other schools.

7.2 The contested points
The many Newar paddhatis still unstudied; the exact rules of combination (which deities
map to which); the relation of the synthesis to the earlier individual traditions.

7.3 What Ochema can contribute
Use the Sarvāmnāya as a stress-test of the crosswalk layer: can the model describe a
deliberate multi-tradition union, not just single traditions? This is a comparative-method
question, not a claim about the tradition.

7.4 What Ochema must not claim yet
That the model "proves" the synthesis's theology, or that the synthesis validates a
particular comparative method.

7.5 Final synthesis
The Sarvāmnāya is the crosswalk layer made tradition: several āmnāyas deliberately united
in Nepal. It is the endgame of the corpus — where the distinct systems are shown to be
mutually translatable, and where the ochema model faces its strongest test.

RESEARCH GAP
The saivamap/sarvamnyaya folder is empty and the Newar paddhatis are unstudied. Before this
workbook can move from "frame" to "reading," the primary material (Newar ritual handbooks,
the cross-āmnāya dossiers) must be added. Until then, it is the most provisional of the
seven.
