PRATYABHIJÑĀ: RECOGNITION
A RECOGNITION-FIRST WORKBOOK
What does it mean to recognize oneself as the Lord?
Why does experience seem to contradict it?
Why does recognition need memory, imagination, and freedom?

PURPOSE
This workbook gives the Pratyabhijñā (recognition) school a logical entry point. It does
not ask you to accept Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics. It reconstructs the questions the system
asks, the answers it proposes, why each concept is introduced, and where the contested
inferences remain.
Prepared as part of the Tantrāloka × Ochema research programme · August 2026

---
HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK
Do not read the Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā as verses to memorize. Read it as one escalating
problem: *if the self is already unrestricted consciousness, why does it present itself as
a bounded, vulnerable, separate person?*
1. Read one unit at a time. The units are cumulative.
2. Keep three columns: what the text claims / what Ochema translates / what remains unproven.
3. Do the checkpoints. The purpose is conceptual control, not memorization.
4. Treat every modern comparison as an analogue, never an identity.
5. Return to the kārikā map only after Units 1–6.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE
Never allow a mathematical theorem, neuroscientific model, or contemplative experience to
inherit the truth of a metaphysical claim merely because their structures resemble one
another. Translation, analogy, implementation, and identity are four different relations.

THE ONE PROBLEM
A self that is already all cannot *learn* to be what it is; it can only *re-cognize* it.
Everything in the Pratyabhijñā is the working-out of that single insight.

UNIT 1 — WHAT IS RECOGNITION?
Recognition (pratyabhijñā) is the system's name and its whole claim. *jñā* = to know;
*prati-* = "return" / "counter." Knowing-again.

1.1 Re-cognition, not new attainment
The aim is not the acquisition of a new fact but the realization of an identity already
present. Ratié, quoting Abhinavagupta's own gloss, makes it precise:

> "The aim of the treatise is a recognition, which is not a new knowledge, but the
> realization that one already possesses a knowledge."
> — Isabelle Ratié, *On reason and scripture in the Pratyabhijñā* (2017), p. 121

1.2 The ordinary structure
Recognition is what happens when a memory returns: you see a person and know, "this is the
same one." A synthesis of present perception with a remembered form — not inference, not
novelty, but the return of identity.

1.3 Recognition has a truth-condition
Recognition succeeds only if the identity is *true*. Confidence, intensity, and
transformation cannot substitute for veridicality. An experience can be transformative
and yet not be recognition of Śiva. (See the Tantrāloka workbook, Unit 1: the same
sharp distinction governs the whole corpus.)

> "An experience can be psychologically transformative and phenomenologically convincing
> without being recognition of Śiva."
> — *Tantrāloka Recognition-First Workbook*, §1.1

CHECKPOINT 1
• What is the "re-" of re-cognition, and why does it matter?
• Why can no intense experience substitute for a *true* recognition?

UNIT 2 — WHY IS RECOGNITION NOT AN INFERENCE?
The distinction blocks a fundamental misreading: that the Pratyabhijñā is a syllogism that
*proves* we are Śiva. It is not. It establishes no new fact.

2.1 The pointing-out, not the proof
> "[The inference] merely leads the subject who hears it to pay attention to his or her
> own experience... it is equivalent to the act of showing that consists in making
> [someone] pay attention [to something by saying] *See! See!*"
> — Ratié, *On reason and scripture*, pp. 121–122 (quoting the IPVV)

2.2 The prefix *prati-* carries the force
> "Abhinavagupta explains that the goal of the treatise is not a cognition but a
> re-cognition (praty-abhijñā), where praty- stands for pratīpam, again... It is a
> re-cognition because the Self's manifestation is not something that did not exist
> before, because its manifestation is never interrupted."
> — Ratié, p. 122 (quoting the IPVV)

2.3 The arguments are de-occluding
The arguments do not add to the self; they redirect attention to the self's never-
interrupted self-manifestation. They are therapeutic, not productive of a new fact.

CHECKPOINT 2
• Why is recognition not an inference?
• What does "See! See!" reveal about the function of the arguments?

UNIT 3 — WHAT MUST CONSCIOUSNESS BE?
For recognition to be possible, consciousness must have three inseparable features.

3.1 Prakāśa — manifestation
Consciousness appears; it is self-luminous. Nothing is known without it. The self is never
absent from awareness.

3.2 Vimarśa — reflexive self-knowing
Consciousness does not merely appear; it knows its own appearing. A light that did not
know its own shining would be a stone, not awareness. The two are inseparable poles.

3.3 Svātantrya — creative freedom
> "Utpaladeva's elaborate phenomenological analyses of memory, imagination, perception,
> intentionality, etc., all point out the boundless creative freedom (svātantrya) of the
> individual's consciousness – a freedom which is the essence of the Lord described by the
> Śaiva scriptures."
> — Ratié, p. 122

The freedom to remember is the freedom by which consciousness manifests a world.

CHECKPOINT 3
• Why is luminosity (prakāśa) alone insufficient?
• How does memory reveal the creative freedom (svātantrya)?

UNIT 4 — HOW DOES LIMITATION ARISE?
If the self is unrestricted, how does it appear as a bounded person?

4.1 Contraction and misidentification
The limitation is not a change of reality but a misidentification: the unrestricted self is
identified with a bounded "me." A failure of recognition, not a new entity.

4.2 The five kañcukas
kalā (limited agency), vidyā (limited knowledge), rāga (attachment), kāla (time), niyati
(necessity) contract infinite capacities into finite experience. The finite subject is a
contracted configuration of consciousness, not a separate thing.

4.3 Not illusion
The world is not dismissed. It is a real manifestation of consciousness. The error is not
that the world exists; it is that it is taken to be outside and other than consciousness.

CHECKPOINT 4
• What is the difference between "becoming limited" and "misidentifying oneself as limited"?
• Why is the world not dismissed?

UNIT 5 — THE A/B/C STRATIFICATION
Liberation-as-recognition is not a single leap. It stratifies.

5.1 A — reflexive presence (established)
Consciousness is always already present to itself. Even before any "I," there is the sheer
luminosity of awareness.

5.2 B — ownership (plausible)
This presence is *mine* — a first-person awareness that owns its states.

5.3 C — universalization (the live frontier)
This very consciousness is the Lord's — the "I" is not a private subject but the self of
all. Here the argument must work hardest, and the honest doubt lives.

Torella captures the whole stratification in one line:

> "Recognition... concerns an entity which, invested from the outset with a valid means of
> knowledge, is already manifest."
> — Raffaele Torella, *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* (critical ed.), introduction

CHECKPOINT 5
• What are the three levels, and which is most contested?
• Why does the stratification keep the teaching honest?

UNIT 6 — WHY CAN RECOGNITION FAIL?
Because consciousness is freedom, no proof can force recognition. This is the nuance that
keeps the teaching razor-sharp.

6.1 Recognition is not guaranteed
> "Reason's power is ultimately limited by the fact that Recognition is in no way
> guaranteed by its use: the most elaborate demonstrations do not necessarily produce the
> realization of one's identity with Śiva, because in a system that defines consciousness
> as an absolute freedom (svātantrya), reason's necessity can only be compelling if
> consciousness agrees to be compelled."
> — Ratié, p. 133

6.2 The consequence
Recognition can be offered, shown, pointed to; it cannot be coerced. The freedom that makes
the self divine is the very freedom that lets it refuse to recognize itself.

CHECKPOINT 6
• Why can't even the best argument force recognition?
• What does the freedom-to-refuse reveal about the premise?

UNIT 7 — WHAT CHANGES IN LIBERATION?
If the self never ceased to be present and free, liberation cannot add anything.

7.1 A correction of identification
Liberation is not attainment but the correction of a misidentification: the finite "me" was
never separate from the consciousness through which everything appears.

7.2 Completion is dṛḍhavimarśa
The goal is a firm, stable reflective awareness — recognition that *holds*. Ratié uses the
term for what reason is *for*:

> "They also assign to reason the task of transforming scriptures... into a means of
> knowledge expressing a strong realization (dṛḍhavimarśa)."
> — Ratié, pp. 132–133

7.3 The lived difference
Though nothing is added, everything is felt differently. The receiver is recognized as the
source.

CHECKPOINT 7
• Why is liberation a correction, not an attainment?
• What is dṛḍhavimarśa, and why does it matter?

UNIT 8 — WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

8.1 The strongest chain
Manifestation is self-showing; self-showing is self-knowing; the self is free; limitation
is misidentification; recognition corrects it. Each step textually anchored.

8.2 The contested points
The C-level (universalization: is "I am all" established or only plausible?); the contested
readings in the IPVV; the exact force of memory across lives.

8.3 What Ochema can contribute
Map recognition onto the grid; compare with modern work on for-me-ness and the
reflexivism/anonymism debate — as correspondence, never identity.

8.4 What Ochema must not claim yet
That cognitive science "proves" recognition, or that the tradition "proves" a cognitive
model.

8.5 Final synthesis
Recognition is the keystone. It defines the vocabulary (prakāśa, vimarśa, svātantrya) that
every other workbook depends on. Get it right, and the Spanda, Krama, and Trika workbooks
all have their anchor.
