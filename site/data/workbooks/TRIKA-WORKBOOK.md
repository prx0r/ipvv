A RECOGNITION-FIRST WORKBOOK

PURPOSE
This workbook gives the Trika tradition a logical entry point. It does not ask you to
accept Kashmir Śaiva metaphysics. It reconstructs the questions the system asks, the
answers it proposes, why each concept is introduced, and where the contested inferences
remain.
Prepared as part of the Tantrāloka × Ochema research programme · August 2026

---
HOW TO USE THIS WORKBOOK
Do not read the Tantrāloka as doctrines to memorize. Read it as one escalating problem:
*if finite experience is already a manifestation of universal consciousness, why does it
present itself as bounded, vulnerable, separate, and only locally powerful?*
1. Read one unit at a time. The units are cumulative.
2. Keep three columns: what the text claims / what Ochema translates / what remains unproven.
3. Do the checkpoints. Conceptual control, not memorization.
4. Treat every modern comparison as an analogue, never an identity.
5. Return to the āhnika map only after Units 1–6.

METHODOLOGICAL RULE
Never allow a mathematical theorem, neuroscientific model, or contemplative experience to
inherit the truth of a metaphysical claim merely because their structures resemble one
another. Translation, analogy, implementation, and identity are four different relations.

THE ONE PROBLEM
If I am already the Lord (consciousness), why does my experience present itself as a small,
limited "me" looking out at a separate world? The Trika is the working-out of that mismatch.

UNIT 1 — WHAT IS THE TRIKA?

1.1 The triad
The Trika is named for its three goddesses — Parā (highest), Parāparā (middle), Aparā
(lower) — assimilated to Kālī systems and philosophically reconstructed on Pratyabhijñā
foundations by Abhinavagupta.

1.2 The triad as structure
The triad models reality as three registers of one consciousness's self-expression. This
is why the Trika can hold the kula/akula polarity without collapsing into dualism.

1.3 The escalating problem
The Trika's core question is the one above: the mismatch between the unlimited self and the
limited experience.

UNIT 2 — WHY THE RECOGNITION PROBLEM FIRST?

2.1 Recognition first
The recognition problem comes first; the 37 āhnikas only become intelligible once it is
clear. The Tantrāloka is a therapy for a misrecognition, not a catalog of beliefs.

2.2 The question chain
What does recognition mean? → an already-existing identity correctly reidentified.
Why don't I experience this? → contraction (saṃkoca), forgetting.
What is consciousness doing? → prakāśa-vimarśa.
How does limitation arise? → the kañcukas, māyā.
How is it undone? → the means: knowledge, yoga, mantra, ritual.

2.3 The truth-condition
Recognition succeeds only when the identity is true. Confidence, intensity, and
transformation cannot replace veridicality. The workbook sharpens it:

> "Ordinary recognition has the form 'this is that.'... The act succeeds only when the
> identity is true. Confidence, intensity, or transformation cannot replace veridicality.
> Recognition = transformation + reidentification + veridicality + stabilization."
> — *Tantrāloka Recognition-First Workbook*, §1.1

And its sharp distinction:
> "A nondual experience is evidence that a self–world organization can change. It is not,
> by itself, proof that one universal consciousness is the ontology of reality."
> — *Tantrāloka Recognition-First Workbook*, §1.1

CHECKPOINT 2
• Why is the recognition problem prior to the āhnikas?
• What is the truth-condition, and why does it keep the teaching honest?

UNIT 3 — WHAT IS CONSCIOUSNESS DOING?

3.1 Prakāśa — appearing
Consciousness appears; it is self-luminous. Nothing is known without it.

3.2 Vimarśa — reflexive self-knowing
Consciousness does not merely appear; it knows its own appearing. A light that did not
know its own shining would be a stone, not awareness. This reflexive pair is the engine.

3.3 The Unsurpassed (anuttara)
Behind all manifest activity is the supreme, as-yet-unsurpassed source from which the
powers (śakti) arise.

UNIT 4 — HOW DOES LIMITATION ARISE?

4.1 The kañcukas
The five coverings contract Śiva's infinite capacities into finite experience: kalā
(limited agency), vidyā (limited knowledge), rāga (attachment), kāla (time), niyati
(necessity). Their result is the localized experiencer (puruṣa).

4.2 Māyā is not illusion
Māyā is the power by which consciousness manifests its own contents as apparently other
than itself. The world is real, but its ultimate nature is not outside consciousness.

4.3 Finitude vs bondage
Finitude = localization; bondage = localization treated as exhaustive separation. To
perceive from somewhere is not to perceive from everywhere.

UNIT 5 — WHAT ARE THE MEANS?

5.1 The upāyas
Each answer creates the next question. The Tantrāloka needs philosophy *and* yoga *and*
mantra *and* initiation *and* ritual *and* aesthetics — each is a means (upāya) suited to a
different configuration of the contraction.

5.2 The intertwined architectures
The means are not separate ladders; they intertwine. Knowledge, yoga, mantra, and ritual
work together because the contraction they undo is complex.

5.3 The caution
That each upāya changes experience is a datum. That it undoes the contraction of
consciousness is the metaphysical claim.

CHECKPOINT 5
• Why does the Trika need multiple means rather than one?
• What does each means address?

UNIT 6 — WHY IS THE TRIKA THE CALIBRATION LAYER?

6.1 The doctrinal oracle
The Trika is the calibration layer. Its Tantrāloka supplies the doctrinal oracle — the
technical vocabulary (prakāśa, vimarśa, svātantrya, kula, spanda) that every other school's
module depends on.

6.2 The Rosetta layer
The Trika's vocabulary makes the Krama, Kubjikā, and Kaula material mutually translatable.
It is the late synthesis that uses the Pratyabhijñā and Spanda currents even as it
supersedes them.

6.3 The role in the corpus
Get the Trika's terms right and the other workbooks (Spanda, Krama, Kubjikā, Kaula,
Sarvāmnāya) all have their semantic anchor.

UNIT 7 — WHAT REMAINS UNRESOLVED?

7.1 The strongest Trika chain
Consciousness is reflexive; it manifests a world; limitation is contraction; the upāyas
undo it; recognition is liberation.

7.2 The contested points
The exact status of the Trika as a sect vs a synthesis; the contested readings within the
37 āhnikas; the kula/akula relation.

7.3 What Ochema can contribute
Map the Trika's vocabulary onto the grid; compare the recognition model with modern work on
self-consciousness and for-me-ness — as correspondence, never identity.

7.4 What Ochema must not claim yet
That cognitive science "proves" the Trika's recognition model, or that the Trika "proves" a
cognitive model.

7.5 Final synthesis
The Trika is the doctrinal oracle and calibration layer. Its vocabulary (prakāśa, vimarśa,
svātantrya, kula, spanda) is presupposed by the Spanda, Krama, Kubjikā, and Kaula workbooks.
Get it right, and the whole corpus becomes navigable.
