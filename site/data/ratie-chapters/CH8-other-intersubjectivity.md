# RATIÉ CH. 8 — THE OTHER IN THE PRATYABHIJÑĀ: SOLIPSISM OR INTERSUBJECTIVITY?
*Per-chapter analysis for the thesis. Source lines 26274–29431. The hardest problem: if
all is the Self, how is the other real? Answer: ontological solipsism but not subjective
solipsism — the other is recognized (not inferred) as the same self-luminous freedom.*

---

## 1. THE CHAPTER'S THESIS
The Pratyabhijñā is an *ontological solipsism* (only absolute consciousness ultimately
exists), yet it is *not a subjective solipsism*: the other is a real self-manifestation, a
subject (pramātṛ) — **recognized** (pratyabhijñā), not inferred, as the same self-luminous
freedom I grasp in myself. This monism *founds* intersubjectivity and compassion.

## 2. EXTENDED QUOTES (French verbatim + English translation)

### Q1 — The problem (lines 26278–26283)
> "Si seul le Soi existe… l'altérité n'est-elle pas pure illusion, et autrui ne se trouve-t-il
> pas réduit à un simulacre aussi peu réel que les personnages qui hantent nos rêves ?"
> **"If only the Self exists… is not alterity pure illusion, and is not the other reduced to
> a simulacrum as unreal as the characters that haunt our dreams?"**

### Q2 — The other is guessed, not inferred (ĪPK 1.1.5, lines 27882, 28721–28753)
> "notre conscience de l'existence d'autrui n'est pas une simple cognition (jñāna), mais une
> reconnaissance (pratyabhijñā)… être conscient de l'existence d'autrui, c'est reconnaître
> hors de soi sa propre subjectivité."
> **"our consciousness of the other's existence is not a simple cognition (jñāna), but a
> recognition (pratyabhijñā)… being conscious of the other's existence is recognizing one's
> own subjectivity outside oneself."**

**→ THE THESIS NODE (second occurrence).** Recognition of the other = re-cognition of my
own subjectivity outside myself. Utpaladeva says the other is "guessed" (ūhyate), NOT
"inferred" (anumīyate) — to avoid reducing the other to an object.

### Q3 — The other resists objectivation (lines 29266–29276)
> "autrui est ce qui résiste à mon acte d'objectivation, la présence dont je prends
> conscience comme ce qui 'ne saurait supporter d'être saisi objectivement'."
> **"the other is that which resists my act of objectivation, the presence I become aware of
> as that which 'could not bear to be grasped objectively'."**

**→ THE FELT.** The other's felt resistance to objectification — the non-objective felt of
the other subject.

### Q4 — Solipsism founds intersubjectivity (lines 29398–29414)
> "si la Pratyabhijñā est un solipsisme… paradoxalement, cette solitude métaphysique n'est
> incompatible ni avec l'altruisme, ni… avec l'intersubjectivité – au contraire : elle la
> fonde."
> **"if the Pratyabhijñā is a solipsism… paradoxically this metaphysical solitude is
> incompatible neither with altruism nor, more generally, with intersubjectivity — on the
> contrary: it *founds* it."**

## 3. IPK LINKS
- **ĪPK 1.1.5** — the central text: knowledge is self-established; action is perceived by
  others; *by that action the other's cognition is guessed (ūhyate)*.
- **ĪPK II 4.4, 4.9** — the potter is Śiva (only consciousness acts).
- **ĪPK I 6.7, I 8.9** — even imagination is authored by absolute consciousness.
- **ĪPK I 5.10** — the spectators fused in watching a dancer (temporary unification).
- **ĪPK III 1.9** — the pañcakañcuka-powers (limited individuals).

## 4. THESIS LITERATURE-REVIEW VALUE
- **The other as recognized** (Q2): a second, crucial occurrence of recognition as the felt
  re-cognition — I *recognize* my subjectivity outside myself. Pairs with the paper's
  intersubjectivity treatment.
- **The felt-of-the-other** (Q3): the other's resistance to objectivation = the non-objective
  felt. Pairs with Solms' non-objective affect (the felt cannot be captured as an object).
- **Co-presence gap** (Stage 1): integration ⟹̸ experienced-together — Ch8 is the tradition's
  own engagement with this.
- **Monism founds intersubjectivity** (Q4): the paper's Part III (consciousness-fundamental
  does not erase the other — it grounds compassion).

## 5. IPVV TRANSLATION HOOKS
- **pratyabhijñā** (recognition) — the other is recognized, not inferred.
- **ūha / ūhana** (guessing) — vs anumāna (inference).
- **pramātṛ** (subject) — the other as subject, not object.
- **svatantra** (autonomous) — the recognized freedom.
- **paratva** (alterity) — denied paramārthatas.
- **pratibimba / bimba** (reflection / source) — the field model.
- **idantāvimarśāsahiṣṇu** — "cannot bear to be grasped objectively" (Q3).
