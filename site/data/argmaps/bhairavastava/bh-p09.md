# Bhairavastava v9: the Lord as unique and covenant-knowing

## 1. What is at issue

BLOCKED — no L1/L2 yet. The Sanskrit attributes qualities to Bhairava: priyam (dear),
sudarśanam (beautiful/good to see), ekam (unique), durlabham (hard to obtain), anyajanaiḥ
(by others), samayajñam (covenant-knowing). The question: is this praise (stotra register)
or ontology (claiming real attributes of the Lord)?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step (from Sanskrit)

1. **Vocative:** tvāṃ priyam api — "You, dear indeed." The api ("indeed") intensifies —
   You are *indeed* dear.

2. **Attributes:** sudarśanam (beautiful to behold) + ekam (unique) — two qualities:
   aesthetic (He is the one worth seeing) and metaphysical (there is no other).

3. **Access:** durlabham anyajanaiḥ — "hard to obtain by others." The uniqueness is
   not just ontological but relational — others cannot reach Him. The devotee can.

4. **Quality:** samayajñam — "knowing the covenant/samaya." Samaya is the tantric
   samaya — the ritual covenant between guru and disciple, or between deity and
   devotee. The Lord *knows* the covenant — He is aware of the agreement.

5. **Conclusion:** Bhairava is unique, beautiful, hard-to-reach, and covenant-knowing.
   The devotee has access that others lack — not through merit but through the samaya.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim exclusivity (only this devotee), nor
that others are excluded from salvation. It claims access through samaya — a covenantal
relationship, not a merit-based one.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. BLOCKED — needs L1/L2. The translation will determine whether samayajñam means
   "knowing the covenant" (knowledge of the agreement) or "knowing the appointed time"
   (kāla-samaya).

2. "Hard to obtain by others" (durlabham anyajanaiḥ) — is this a factual claim about
   others' inability, or a devotional intensification ("so rare that others cannot")?
   The Sanskrit allows both.
