# Bhairavastava v7: nondifference manifests through the hymn

## 1. What is at issue

The verse claims: tad eva mama tvadabhedam — "that alone, my nondifference with You."
But this nondifference is not claimed directly — it arrives through (or with) the hymn:
stotraparāmṛtavṛṣṭir udeti — "the supreme-nectar rain of the hymn rises." The question:
does the hymn *cause* nondifference, or does nondifference *manifest when* the hymn arises?
The verse says "tadaiva" — "then alone" — suggesting a condition, not a cause.

Dyczkowski: "When this trouble arises... it brings about in him a rain of nectar, that supreme nectar of consciousness."

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (condition):** tadaiva — "then alone" (only when). The nondifference is
   conditional on the hymn's arising. Without the hymn, the nondifference is not claimed.

2. **Premise (the hymn):** stotraparāmṛtavṛṣṭi — "supreme-nectar rain of the hymn."
   The hymn is not a request but a substance (nectar-rain) — it has ontological weight.

3. **Inference:** abheda (nondifference) is not a philosophical doctrine imported from
   outside but an *effect* (or correlate) of the hymn's arising. The stotra is the
   condition under which the devotee's nondifference-with-Bhairava becomes manifest.

4. **Conclusion:** The hymn performs nondifference. It does not describe what is already
   the case — it is the means by which the case becomes true for the devotee.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim universal metaphysical nondifference
(advaya). It claims the devotee's nondifference with Bhairava as conditional on the hymn.
This is bhakti-advaya, not viśiṣṭādvaya.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. "Then alone" (tadaiva) — does this mean the nondifference lasts only while the hymn
   is arising (ephemeral), or that it *begins* then (permanent)? The verse's tense
   (udeti, present) suggests ongoing — the hymn-rain is continuous.

2. The metaphor — is the hymn-rain a vehicle (it carries nondifference) or a catalyst
   (it enables it)? Both readings fit "udeti arisearises."

3. Dyczkowski (lecture) reveals the MECHANISM: kleśa-dasha (affliction-state) is the TRIGGER, not the obstacle. "The minute he fills this body of troubles, immediately inspires rain of nectar." Trouble springs grace — the affliction and the nectar are one movement.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is conditional (nondifference through hymn). L2 should preserve
"then alone" (the conditionality) and the hymn-as-substance reading, without universalizing
the nondifference claim.
