# Bhairavastava v10: the compassionate Lord pacifies the world's heat instantly

## 1. What is at issue

The verse claims: yena vibhur bhavamarusantāpaṃ śamayati jhaṭiti janasya dayāluḥ — "by whom
the Lord, the world's great heat, pacifies instantly, the compassionate one." This is an
efficacy claim: the Lord's compassion has immediate (jhaṭiti, "in a flash") effect on the
world's suffering (aru-santāpa, "terrible heat"). The question: what makes this pacification
instant, and what is the "heat" it pacifies?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (the world's condition):** bhavamarusantāpa — "the world's great burning
   heat." The metaphor is universal: saṃsāra as fever/heat. This is the most expansive
   suffering-claim in the stotra (v3 named personal suffering; this is cosmic).

2. **Premise (the Lord's action):** śamayati — "pacifies/soothes." Not destroys, not
   transcends — soothes. The heat is not annihilated but cooled.

3. **Premise (speed):** jhaṭiti — "in a flash, instantly." The pacification is not
   gradual. This is the stotra's most radical temporal claim.

4. **Premise (condition):** yena — "by whom" — the pacification occurs *by* the Lord's
   action. The causal agent is external (the Lord), not the devotee's effort.

5. **Inference:** dayāluḥ — "compassionate" — compassion is the *means* of pacification.
   The Lord's nature (compassion) produces the effect (pacification), not a ritual or
   method.

6. **Conclusion:** The Lord's compassionate nature instantly pacifies cosmic suffering —
   without method, without delay, without effort from the devotee. This closes the
   stotra's arc: from personal surrender (v1) to cosmic compassion (v10).

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim the world *will be* pacified eschatologically.
The present tense (śamayati) and the immediacy (jhaṭiti) suggest a perennial present — the
Lord is always pacifying, always compassionate.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. bhavamarusantāpa — "the world's great heat" — is this the heat of saṃsāra (suffering),
   tapas (austerity), or moha (delusion)? The verse does not specify. The stotra's context
   (v3: suffering/delusion/terror) suggests saṃsāra-as-fever.

2. "Instantly" (jhaṭiti) — instantaneous pacification of *cosmic* suffering seems to
   contradict the stotra's own acknowledgment of ongoing suffering (v3). Either the verse
   means "instantly available" (a standing offer) or "instantly effective when the devotee
   is in the relationship established by v1-v9."

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is the stotra's cosmic close. L2 should preserve the immediacy
(jhaṭiti → "in a flash"), the causal structure (by compassion, not by method), and the
tension with v3 (suffering acknowledged, suffering pacified) — without resolving it.
