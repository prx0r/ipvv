# Bhairavastava v2: You are my own-self, all is Yours

## 1. What is at issue

BLOCKED — no L1/L2 yet. The Sanskrit claims ontological identity: tvaṃ ca maheśa — "You,
O Lord" — sadaiva mamā'tmā — "always my own-self" — svātmamayaṃ mama tena samastam — "your-
self-made, all of that is mine." The question: is this identity claim metaphysical (I am You)
or devotional (You are mine)? And how does it square with the bowing in v1?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step (from Sanskrit)

1. **Address:** tvaṃ ca maheśa — "and You, O Lord." The ca ("and") connects to v1 —
   after bowing, the devotee now addresses Bhairava directly.

2. **Claim:** sadaiva mamā'tmā — "always my own-self." The sadaiva ("always") is
   temporal-universal — not sometimes, not eventually. The self-identification is
   perennial.

3. **Compound:** svātmamayaṃ — "made of Your own-self." The devotee's self is made
   *of* Bhairava's self. This is more than relationship — it's constituent identity.

4. **Possession:** mama tena samastam — "mine, from that, all." Whatever is Bhairava's
   is the devotee's — and vice versa. The relationship is bidirectional.

5. **Conclusion:** The devotee claims ontological kinship with Bhairava — not as
   philosophical realization (vimarśa) but as given condition (sadaiva, "always").

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim the devotee IS Bhairava (aham śiva).
It claims svātmamaya — the self is made of His self. The grammar preserves a subject-
object distinction even while claiming constitutive identity.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. BLOCKED — needs L1/L2. The translation will determine whether samastam ("all") refers
   to all possessions or all reality.

2. sadaiva ("always") — does this mean the identity was always the case (metaphysical),
   or the devotee's recognition of it is always the case (epistemic)? The Sanskrit is
   ambiguous.

## 4. Decision for L2

HOLD — cannot proceed without L1. The Sanskrit claim is significant (constitutive identity
+ bidirectional possession) and must not be translated without careful glossing of
svātmamaya and samastam.
