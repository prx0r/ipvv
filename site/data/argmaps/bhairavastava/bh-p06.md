# Bhairavastava v6: bliss as entering the fullness of You

## 1. What is at issue

The verse claims: tvayi ahaṃ ātmani nirvṛtim emi — "in You, I, the self, enter bliss
(nirvṛti)." What is nirvṛti here? Is it cessation (niḥ + vṛti, "without movement"), bliss,
or liberation? And who enters what — does the self enter the Lord, or enter a state?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (condition):** bhāvaparāmṛtanirbharapūrṇe — "in the One full of the
   supreme nectar of being." The condition is being-full (pūrṇatā) in the Lord's bhāva
   (state/being). This is the same ontological fullness claimed in v1.

2. **Premise (subject):** tvayi aham ātmani — "in You, I, in my self." The subject is
   the devotional self, not the universal ātman — the possessive "my" (asmātman) keeps
   it devotional.

3. **Inference:** nirvṛtim emi — "I enter nirvṛti." The verb emi (go/enter) implies
   movement toward, not static being. The self *enters* a state it was not already in.

4. **Conclusion:** Bliss is not discovered but entered — through the condition of
   being-full in the Lord. The entrance is the result of the fullness (pūrṇatā), not
   independent of it.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim the self *is* the Lord (identity), nor
that nirvṛti = mokṣa. It claims entry into a state conditioned by fullness-in-You.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. nirvṛti vs. ananda — the L1 glosses it "bliss" but niḥ+vṛti literally means
   "without movement" (cessation). The Trika reading often equates the two, but the
   verse's grammar (entering, not abiding) complicates a static reading.

2. ātmani — is this "in my self" (devotional self) or "in the Self" (universal)? The
   asmātman in the compound suggests devotional, but Trika texts routinely operate on
   both levels simultaneously.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is a result-claim about entry into bliss through fullness-in-You.
L2 should preserve the movement verb (enter) and the conditional structure (through
fullness), without equating nirvṛti with a specific doctrine (mokṣa, kaivalya, etc.).
