# Bhairavastava v4: service and contemplation produce power

## 1. What is at issue

What is the mechanism of transformation? The verse names two practices — sevana (service
of Śaṅkara) and cintanā (contemplation) — and claims they produce a specific result: the
devotee becomes bhīṣaṇabhairavaśaktimaya ("filled with terrifying Bhairava-power"). The
question is: how does service + contemplation yield power, and what kind of power is it?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (first practice):** śaṅkarasevana — service of Śaṅkara. Not ritual alone
   but a relationship of service (sevana implies ongoing, not one-time).

2. **Premise (second practice):** cintanādhīra — firm in contemplation. The qualifier
   "firm" (dhīra) suggests this is not casual reflection but stabilized meditation.

3. **Premise (state):** The two practices are not sequential but simultaneous — the
   ablative "by Śaṅkara-service" and the locative "in contemplation-firm" describe
   concurrent conditions.

4. **Inference:** From these concurrent practices, the devotee becomes bhīṣaṇabhairava-
   śaktimaya — filled with the terrifying Bhairava-power. The power is not acquired;
   the devotee becomes *made of it* (-maya suffix).

5. **Conclusion:** Service and contemplation are not devotional ornament — they are the
   mechanism by which the devotee's substance transforms.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim the devotee gains Bhairava's powers
(siddhis). It claims the devotee's *substance* becomes power-filled — a change of nature,
not a grant of capability.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. bhīṣaṇa ("terrifying") — terrifying to whom? The verse does not specify. In Trika
   context, bhīṣaṇa often refers to Bhairava's fierce aspect that destroys obstacles
   (āvaraṇas), but the verse is silent on this.

2. The causal structure — does sevanā produce cintanā, or do both independently produce
   the result? The grammar (two instrumentals, one result) is ambiguous.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is methodological (practice → result). L2 should name the two practices
and the specific result (power-filled substance), without over-specifying what "terrifying
power" means doctrinally.
