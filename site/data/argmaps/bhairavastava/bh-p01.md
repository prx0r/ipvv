# Bhairavastava v1: the devotional stance

## 1. What is at issue

How does a devotee approach Bhairava? The verse establishes the opening move of the entire
stotra: total surrender of mind. The question is not "who is Bhairava?" but "what is the
devotee's posture before Bhairava?" — and the answer is ontological, not behavioral: the
mind has *become* His (tvanmayacittatayā), it is not merely directed at Him.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (the Lord's status):** Bhairavanātha is anāthaśaraṇya — the refuge of those
   without refuge. This is not an attribute but a definition: He is the one to whom the
   helpless have nowhere else to turn.

2. **Premise (the devotee's state):** tvanmayacittatayā — "by mind-become-Yours." The mind
   is not seeking; it has already been transformed. The locative-with-causal force suggests
   the transformation is complete before the veneration begins.

3. **Inference:** If the mind is already His, then hṛdi vande ("in heart, I venerate") is
   not an action but a recognition — the heart recognizes what the mind has become.

4. **Conclusion:** The stotra opens not with a request but with a statement of achieved
   union. The devotee is already inside the relationship.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim philosophical identity with Bhairava —
it claims devotional identity (tvanmaya, "made of You"). The distinction matters: this is
bhakti grammar, not vimarśa grammar.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. tvanmayacittatayā — literally "by-mind-of-Your-substance-ness." Is this a state achieved
   prior to the stotra, or the stotra's own effect? The verse reads as achieved, but the
   stotra genre implies the hymn performs the transformation.

2. hṛdi ("in heart") — the heart as the locus of veneration is conventional (hṛdaya = the
   innermost). No anatomical claim is made, but the locative could be read as locative of
   place or locative of instrument (by means of the heart).

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is devotional, not argumentative — the "argument" is the devotional
stance itself. L2 should preserve the achieved-state reading (mind already His) without
over-interpreting tvanmaya as philosophical identity.
