## bh-p01 — argument map

The object is fixed in full: all-pervading (vyāpta) particular of moving-and-unmoving states (carācarabhāvaviśeṣa), consisting of consciousness (cinmaya), one (eka), endless (ananta), beginningless (anādi). The act follows: Bhairava-Lord (bhairavanātham), refuge of the helpless (anāthaśaraṇyaṃ), venerated by mind-become-Yours (tvanmayacittatayā), in heart (hṛdi) — vande (I venerate). Veneration is not ritual offering but mental absorption: the devotee's mind becoming His (tvanmaya). The name Bhairava performs its own nirukta here: bearing/supporting the universe, the Roar (rava) of AHAM, benefactor of the terrified — all three senses lived in the single act of absorption (Tantrāloka 1.95-100). Total surrender is sized to its object: the all-pervading consciousness is venerated by absorption into it, not by offering to it.

### Open
- Whether 'carācarabhāvaviśeṣa' means particular states or the particularity of states is unresolved.
- Whether 'anādi' negates temporal beginning only or all limitation is unresolved.
- The nirukta is performed, not stated; the verse does not gloss its own name.
