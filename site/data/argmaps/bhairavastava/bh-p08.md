# Bhairavastava v8: contemplating scripture produces the bliss-stream

## 1. What is at issue

The verse claims: tāvakaśāstra-parāmṛta-cintā syandati cetasi nirvṛtidhārām — "contemplation
of your scripture's supreme nectar causes the bliss-stream to flow in the mind." What is
the relationship between scripture (śāstra), contemplation (cintā), and the resulting bliss
(nirvṛti-dhāra)? Is scripture the *source* of bliss, or the *occasion*?

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (the scripture):** tāvakaśāstra — "Your scripture." The possessive tāvaka
   ("yours") links the scripture to Bhairava himself — it is not an external text but
   His own expression.

2. **Premise (the practice):** parāmṛta-cintā — "contemplation of supreme nectar."
   The contemplation is not of doctrine but of *nectar* (āmṛta) — the scripture is
   tasted, not analyzed.

3. **Inference:** syandati — "flows." The bliss-stream (nirvṛtidhārā) *flows* — it is
   not produced but released, as from a spring. The contemplation opens the channel.

4. **Conclusion:** Scripture-contemplation (cintā, not adhyayana/study) produces the
   flow of bliss. The verb syandati implies a natural, gravity-driven flow — the
   scripture is the spring, not the pump.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim scripture is the *only* source of bliss,
nor that all scripture produces bliss. It claims contemplation (cintā) of *this* scripture
(śāstra, "yours") produces a specific result.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. "Your scripture" (tāvakaśāstra) — which scripture? The Āgama? The Tantrāloka? The
   verse does not specify, and "yours" could mean "the one belonging to Bhairava" (i.e.,
   the tantric canon) or "the scripture that is You" (i.e., the Lord as scripture).

2. cintā (contemplation) vs. adhyayana (study) — the verse uses cintā, not adhyayana.
   The distinction matters: contemplation implies absorption, not intellectual study.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is methodological (scripture-contemplation → bliss-flow). L2 should
name the practice as contemplation (not study), preserve the "your scripture" possessive,
and keep the flow metaphor intact.
