# Bhairavastava v5: salutation removes fear

## 1. What is at issue

The devotee names the ultimate threats — mṛtyu (death), Yama (lord of death), antaka (the
ender), and karmapiśāca (karma-ghosts) — and claims: namo'stu na jātu bibhemi ("salutation
to the Lord, never do I fear"). The question is: how does salutation (namas) produce
fearlessness? This is a claim about the efficacy of devotion against mortality.

Dyczkowski: "This expanding consciousness free of thought — when thought makes consciousness contract." The practice context: peaceful mind first, then fearlessness.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise (the threats):** Four named threats — death, Yama, the ender, karma-ghosts.
   The list escalates: death (mṛtyu) is the event, Yama is its administrator, antaka is
   its agent, karmapiśāca is its karmic residue. This is total coverage of mortality.

2. **Premise (the response):** namo'stu — "let salutation be." Not mantra, not yoga,
   not ritual — namas (bowing, submission) is the named response.

3. **Inference:** The particle na jātu ("never at all") is absolute — the fearlessness
   is not probabilistic but categorical. Namas, directed at nātha (the Lord), produces
   total fearlessness against the full spectrum of mortality.

4. **Conclusion:** Devotion (specifically namas) has ontological efficacy against death.
   The claim is not that one survives death, but that one does not fear it.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim immortality (amṛtatva) or liberation
(mokṣa). It claims fearlessness — a psychological/soteriological state, not an ontological
change. The distinction is precise: na bibhemi ≠ ahaṃ na mriye.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. Four threats or one? The list (mṛtyu, Yama, antaka, karmapiśāca) could be four
   distinct entities or four names for death's aspects. The verse treats them as
   distinct (separate accusatives) but the tradition often collapses them.

2. "Never at all" (na jātu) — this is strong. Does the devotee not fear death *in this
   moment* (current state) or *ever* (permanent state)? The present tense of the verb
   suggests current, but namas' efficacy implies permanence.

3. Dyczkowski (lecture) cautions: "Bhairava gives freedom from fear, BUT IT TAKES TIME" — the fearlessness is not instant but develops through practice (mind free of thought). This tempers the absolute na jātu: the text claims it, the teacher notes it is a destination, not a starting point.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is an efficacy claim about namas against mortality. L2 should preserve
the absolute force of "never at all fear" without adding doctrinal claims about what
follows (mokṣa, immortality).
