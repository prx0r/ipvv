# Bhairavastava v3: suffering acknowledged

## 1. What is at issue

The verse confronts a direct tension: the devotee has just declared total surrender (v1),
yet suffering is acknowledged as real — durdharaduḥkhavi (hard-to-bear suffering), moha
(delusion), and bhayanāśa (terror-causing karma-hosts). Does devotion deny suffering? This
verse says no: suffering is present, and the devotee does not flinch.

## 2. The argument, reconstructed step by step

1. **Premise:** The conditions are real — satsvapi ("even though present"): hard suffering,
   delusion, and terrifying karmic consequences exist and are acknowledged.

2. **Premise:** The devotee's response is not denial but non-flinching — the verse states
   the conditions without qualifying them.

3. **Inference:** Bhairavanātha's refuge (from v1) does not remove the conditions; it
   changes the devotee's relationship to them. The refuge is not escape but orientation.

4. **Conclusion:** Devotion is compatible with unflinching realism about suffering. The
   stotra's opening move (v1) is not wish-fulfillment — it's a stance that holds both
   surrender and acknowledged pain simultaneously.

**What is NOT argued:** The verse does not claim suffering is illusory (māyā), nor that
it will end. It claims only that the devotee does not flinch.

## 3. Unresolved / uncertain (not invented)

1. The three conditions (duḥkha, moha, bhaya-karma) — are they a list (three separate
   things) or a causal chain (suffering → delusion → karmic terror)? The verse lists them
   without connectives; the grammar suggests simultaneity, not sequence.

2. "Even though present" (satsvapi) — this particle does concessive work: "despite X, not-Y."
   The "not-Y" (non-flinching) is implied rather than stated. What exactly the devotee does
   NOT do is left implicit.

## 4. Decision for L2

Proceed. The verse is a concession structure — suffering present, flinching absent. L2
should preserve the concession ("even though...") without adding a doctrinal claim about
why non-flinching occurs.
