# C1 — Tantrasāra Ahnika 11 (śaktipāta)

## tsa11-p01 — why initiation must be taught
**SUMMARY.** This passage frames the whole chapter. Everything taught so far serves some people directly for liberation — they hear the compendium and are free. Others can only use it once initiation is on the table. So initiation, and what follows from it, must now be taught. And the first question is eligibility: who is entitled to receive it? That question is not procedural (who may be initiated) but causal (what makes one capable of receiving it) — and it forces the topic of this ahnika: the descent of power. Nothing is yet asserted about grace; the question is opened, not answered, and the pūrvapakṣa follows.

**FUNCTION.** Frame-setting and topic-introduction. Establishes why śaktipāta, not initiation itself, is the ahnika's subject, and why eligibility is a causal question.

**KEY TERMS.** adhikārin (entitled recipient), apavarga (liberation), saṃgraha-nīti (compendium manner), vaktavyam (must be taught), vicāryate (is examined).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** Opening of the ahnika; follows A10's kalādhvā (digit-course) and precedes the pūrvapakṣa in P2.

**EXPLANATION.** The passage has two sentences and one question. First, the frame: what was taught so far reaches some directly for liberation in the stated compendium manner; for others, it reaches only through coming into use in the initiation to be taught. Hence initiation and the rest must be taught. The "and the rest" (dīkṣādikam) signals that initiation is the door to a whole sequence (snāna, yāga, putraka, sadyaḥ, parokṣa, liṅgoddhāra, abhiṣeka, antyeṣṭi, conduct) that follows in A12–A20. Second, the question: "who is the entitled one?" (kaḥ adhikārī) — and for that determination (nirūpaṇārtham), the descent of power is examined (śaktipāto vicāryate). The shift from "who may be initiated" to "what makes one capable" is the ahnika's pivot: eligibility is not a permission but a causal capacity, and the cause of that capacity is śaktipāta. Nothing is yet asserted about grace; the question is opened and the pūrvapakṣa (three theories) follows in P2.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** No doctrine stated; the pūrvapakṣa theories follow in P2. No unresolved items beyond the frame.
