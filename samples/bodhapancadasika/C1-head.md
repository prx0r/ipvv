# C1 — Bodhapañcadaśikā

## bod-p01–p06 — the one reality and its play
**SUMMARY.** The stotra opens with the one reality: un-set sun-light-form of powers and darknesses — who is one within, whose end are powers and darknesses. He alone is own-nature of all beings, supreme Lord; the host of things is of him alone power, Lordship-made. Power does not desire difference from power-possessor; their identity is eternal, as fire and burning. He alone is Bhairava god, world-bearing-characterized; in own-self-vision the whole is reflected by power. Of him alone is supreme goddess, own-nature-grasping-eager; fullness in all beings, neither little nor excess. This god by this goddess always play-joy-eager; varied creation-dissolution he does simultaneously, all-pervading. The teaching: one reality (Bhairava) whose nature is own-nature (svabhāva), with power (śakti) eternally identical to him (like fire and burning), reflecting the whole world in self-vision, with the supreme goddess as own-nature-grasping-eager fullness — and his play is simultaneous creation and dissolution, all-pervading.

**FUNCTION.** Opens the stotra: establishes the one reality (Bhairava = svabhāva), power-possessor identity, supreme goddess, and the Lord's play (simultaneous creation-dissolution).

**KEY TERMS.** svabhāva (own-nature), parameśvara (supreme Lord), śakti (power), bhairava (Bhairava), parā devī (supreme goddess), svarūpāmarśana (own-nature-grasping), krīḍā (play), sṛṣṭisaṃhāra (creation-dissolution).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** First six verses of the stotra; sets the doctrinal foundation.

**EXPLANATION.** The passage moves in six steps. (1) Un-set-sun-light-form: powers and darknesses have one reality within (anastamitabhārūpa); who is one within, whose end are powers and darknesses — the one reality encompasses both light and darkness. (2) He alone is own-nature of all beings, supreme Lord; the host of things is His power, Lordship-made (sa eva sarvabhūtānāṃ svabhāvaḥ parameśvaraḥ; bhāvajātaṃ hi tasyaiva śaktir īśvaratāmayī). (3) Power does not desire difference from power-possessor (śaktiś ca śaktimadrūpād vyatirekam na vāñchati); identity eternal, as fire and burning (tādātmyam anayor nityaṃ vahnidāhikayor iva). (4) He alone is Bhairava, world-bearing; in own-self-vision whole reflected by power (svātmādṛṣe samagraṃ hi yacchaktyā pratibimbitam). (5) Of him alone is supreme goddess, own-nature-grasping-eager; fullness in all beings, neither little nor excess (tasyaivaiṣā parā devī svarūpāmarśanotsukā; pūrṇatvaṃ sarvabhaveṣu yasyā nālpaṃ na cādhikam). (6) This god by this goddess always play-joy-eager; varied creation-dissolution simultaneously, all-pervading (eṣa devo 'nayā devyā nityaṃ krīḍārasotsukaḥ; vicitrān sṛṣṭisaṃhārān vidhatte yugapad vibhuḥ).
