# C1 — Anuttarāṣṭikā

## anu-p01 — the frame: no method, just staying
**SUMMARY.** Transition here is no method at all — not contemplation, not speech-connection, not discussion, not visualization, not steadying, not japa-practice-effort. So what is well-decided? Speak the supreme truth, and hear it: not renouncer, not possessor — enjoy happiness as staying. The teaching opens by ruling out every technique and landing on a single stance: stay, and enjoy. This is Abhinava's early devotional voice — the stotra form, not the commentary form — and it sets the tone for the whole eight-verse poem: negation of method, then direct statement of the unsurpassed.

**FUNCTION.** Frame-setting and topic-introduction. Rules out all techniques (contemplation, speech, discussion, visualization, steadying, japa) and lands on staying/enjoying.

**KEY TERMS.** saṃkrāma (transition), bhāvanā (contemplation), dhyāna (visualization), dhāraṇā (steadying), japābhyāsa (japa-practice), suniścita (well-decided), paraṃ satya (supreme truth), tyāgī (renouncer), parigrahī (possessor), yathāvasthita (as staying).

**LOCAL CONTEXT.** First verse of the stotra; sets the frame for all eight.

**EXPLANATION.** The verse moves in two parts. First, the negation: transition (saṃkrāma) here is not contemplation (bhāvanā), not connection-of-speech (kathāyukti), not discussion (carcā), not visualization (dhyāna), not steadying (dhāraṇā), not japa-practice-effort (japābhyāsaprayāsa). Six techniques, all ruled out — the stotra's opening gesture is to clear the field of method. Second, the positive: "that — what name? — well-decided, speak the supreme truth, and that hear: not renouncer, not possessor — enjoy happiness as staying." The teaching is not renunciation (tyāga) and not possession (parigraha); it is staying (yathāvasthita) and enjoying (bhaja sukham). The "well-decided" (suniścita) marks this as Abhinava's own settled position, not a tentative suggestion.

**BOUNDARY / OPEN.** No unresolved items; the stotra is self-contained. The "transition" (saṃkrāma) is not explained further — it is the poem's own term for the movement from method to staying.
