## The svācchandya (the spontaneity) of the Lord

Here, again, the blue and the rest — [being] the not-abandoned blue-form and the rest —
manifesting, are of the non-difference-from-the-manifestation (prakāśābheda-maya). And this,
indeed, is the Lord's spontaneity (svācchandya) — that, being of the essence of consciousness
(saṃvid-ātmaka), by the form of the manifold knowers-and-knowns (vicitra-pramātṛ-prameya), even
[being] non-exceeding-the-consciousness-form (saṃvid-rūpānadhika), by the excess-of-the-
consciousness-form (saṃvid-rūpādhikatā), with the body-not-shining-as-separate
(nirbhāsamāna-vapuṣā), shines (prakāśate), and, so, reflects (vimṛśati) too. The shining of
the non-excess [as if] the excess — this is called the transparent-nature (svaccha-bhāva), like
the mirror's. For it (the mirror), by the varieties of the wall, the house, the elephant, the
horse, the man, the pot, the potter — and by the part-whole-ness, the support-supported-ness,
the cause-effect-ness, and the rest, that are theirs — in the host of the practical-activities
(vyavahāra-nivaha), even [being] not-at-all-exceeding-its-own-form, [yet] as-if-exceeding
(atiriktair-iva), whatever it shows, [so] it is called "transparent (svaccha)." But, of it, from
the non-being-of-the-such-reflexion (tathāvimarśābhāva), [there is] the non-spontaneity
(asvācchandya), [i.e.] the inert-ness (jaḍatva). But the supreme Lord, [being] the
manifestation-reflexion's-limit (saṃvido vimarśa-paryantatvāt), reflects (vimṛśati) himself
[even] as-so-manifesting.

And that which was said — "[if] it were always manifesting thus" — there, "always (sarvadā)"
[and] "sometimes (kadācit)": if some time [were] independent-of-the-making-of-it
(tannirmāṇa-nirapekṣa), [being] free (svatantra), by the dependence-on-that (tad-avalambana),
even [he] might be objected. But [it is] not thus. For, in this time, of this word, [whose]
meaning-is-the-illumined (dyotya), [there is] no other meaning apart from the so-manifesting
pulsation-of-the-sun (sūrya-parispandita). This would be said — "why does the blue-and-the-rest
world shine, in the co-existence of the sun-pulsation-and-the-rest?" And this, too, why [is it]
not objected — "why is not the blue itself [seen as] the yellow?" **Objection (nanu):** "Thus,
the blue would not have-been-manifested." If [it is] thus, in the other-wise-shining, that very
sun-pulsation would be not-at-all-not-shining. For, in the not-shining of the seven-oceans'
closing (saptārṇava-mīlana), indeed, on the day-of-dissolution, today's-day [alone] would be,
[and] not today's day, in its being, [as] the both-natured [day].

And, by the separate body (atirikta-vapuṣā), the supreme Lord does not shine — so, here, [there
is] nothing not-evident (aprātītika). For, of the sensation (saṃvedana) itself, in the
construction of the dream, the conception, the śāstra, and the rest, [and] even in the
external-activity (bahiṣkriyā) of the mass-of-beings-established-in-this-and-that-inner
(tattad-antarvya-vasthita), the non-excess (anatireka) is seen. On-the-contrary (pratyuta), the
beyond-the-manifestation-constructed-conception (pratītibahiṣkṛta-parikalpana) [is] not-evident.

Thus — "always (śaśvat)": the non-possibility-of-the-time-limitation (kāla-paricchedāsambhava).
"Uncovered (acchanna)": the non-excess-of-the-own-form (svarūpānatireka). "Transparent (svaccha)":
even in the non-excess, the shining-of-the-excess (atirikta-bhāsa-sambhava). "Free (svacchanda)":
the possession-of-the-reflexion-freedom (vimarśa-svātantrya-yoga). By the word "activity
(vṛtti)," of these forms, in the Lord Śiva, [who is] the power-possessor (śaktimati), the
shining-of-the-self-identity (tādātmyāvabhāsa) is said.

"Always (śaśvat)": not-limited-by-time. "Uncovered": not-partaking-in-any-way-of-the-otherness-
of-the-own-form (svarūpānyathābhāva). "Transparent": bearing, as-it-were, the exceeding-
manifestation (adhika-nirbhāsana). "Free": free-from-the-dependence-characterized-by-the-
seeking-of-the-other-reflexion-station (tad-vimarśa-sthānāntara-upayācñā-lakṣaṇa-pāratantrya-
viyukta). [He] whose activity (vṛtti), [being] the abiding (vartana), [is] the throbbing-of-the-
own-form (svarūpa-spandita) alone — he, indeed, [being] the-excluded-form-of-the-worldly-
delusion's-removal (aśiva-rūpa-sāṃsārika-moha-apahastana-yoga), [and], by the
purpose-of-the-supreme-subordinate-good (para-apara-śreyaḥ-paramārthatā), [being] of the
essence of the bliss-enjoyment-and-release (ānanda-sāra-bhoga-apavarga-rūpa) — he [is] Śiva,
the supreme Lord. To him — [with] "homage (namaḥ)" — the author manifests the prostration of
the speech, the mind, the body (vāṅ-manaḥ-kāyānāṃ prahvatā).

And the prostration [is]: by the seeing-of-the-supreme-excellence-of-the-Lord's-own-form
(bhagavatsvarūpotkarṣa-darśana), [and] by the throwing-off (apāsana) of the pride-of-the-
knower-ness-in-the-body-and-the-rest (śarīrādi-gata-pramātṛtā-abhimāna-udreka), [which is]
conjectured-at-the-time-of-the-arising (vyutthāna-samaya-saṃbhāvyamāna), [and whose] remaining-
activity (śeṣa-vṛtti) [is] of the essence of the impression-of-the-conceived-knower-ness-made-
weak-by-his-will (tad-icchā-vaśa-viśīrṇa), — the entry (samāveśa) [is] of the form of the
sinking (unamagnatā) into the consciousness-natured-own-form (saṃvid-ātmaka-svasvarūpatā), [and]
the surpassing-of-the-māyā-knower-pride (māyāpramātra-abhimāna-atireka). Such [is] the Śiva-
own-form. [Thus:] "as the principal-knower (pradhāna-pramātṛ), I rise-above (adhiśaye), I
lower (nyakkaromi) the māyā-knower-being-pride (māyāpramātṛ-bhāva-abhimāna)" — like the-other-
particle (anya-nipāta), this "homage (namaḥ)" word [is] the manifestor (dyotaka).

But that which is said — the "homage" word's own-form-primary-ness (svarūpa-paratva) — that [is]
not pleasing (manorama). For, by the offering-of-the-own-form-of-the-word to the Lord (bhagavati
namaḥśabda-svarūpārpaṇa), nothing [is done]. By the Gītā-maxim (gītā-nyāya), for the heard
"homage" word, in the Lord, the contentment (paritoṣa) [is] produced — this is the
oath-trustworthy (śapatha-śraddheya) meaning. Of the svāhā-and-the-rest, too, in the oblation
(homa) and the rest, the meaning [is] the increase-of-the-splendor, the offering-to-the-deity,
the satisfaction-and-the-rest (dīpty-abhivṛddhi, devatārpaṇa, tarpaṇa) — not the
own-form-establishedness (svarūpaniṣṭhatā), like the-other-particle (ādi-nipāta). In the
own-form-primary-ness, "and (caḥ)" [would be] read, like [in the case of] "itavat." In the
being-of-the-being-nature (sattva-rūpatā), the non-transformation (anavyayatva) would be, from
the connection-of-the-linga-number (liṅga-saṃkhyādi-yoga-prasaṅga). And, of the object, with the
word, what [is] the connection (yoga)? In that connection, the fourth [case-ending] (caturthī)
would break.

"Homage-is-made (namaskaroti), does-homage (namasyati)" — i.e. "utters the 'homage' word" — in
the termination-of-the-meaning (artha-paryavasāna), "to the supreme Lord (parameśvaram)" — the
external-object-connection (bāhya-karma-saṃbandha) [would be] not-possible. And, then, the
homage [would be] the verbal (vācika) only, or the mental (mānasa) too — so how [is] the
threefold-establishedness (traividhya-prasiddhi), the bodily (kāyika) homage? In our position,
by the mind, the speech, the body, making-oneself-prostrate (svātmānaṃ prahvīkurvan), lowering
(nyagbhāvayan), exalting-the-Lord (bhagavantam utkarṣayan) — [this] is the "homage's" meaning;
so all [is] established. As he will say — "by the naming-of-the-victory-homage-and-the-rest
(jayanamaskārādi-abhidhānena), the Lord's exaltation (īśvarotkarṣa) [is] to-be-shown, and
one's-own-inclination-toward-it (svātmanaś ca tat-pravaṇatā)."

And, in the āgamas, of the namaḥ, the svāhā, the vauṣaḍ, and the rest, [being]
class-word-signified (jāti-śabda-vācya), [and] useful-for-the-generation-of-the-manifold-
attainments (vividha-siddhi-janana-upayogitā), [there is] the being-the-manifestor-of-the-
this-and-that-meaning (tattadartha-dyotakatva) alone — [this] is said. Disregarding-the-existing-
object-service (sambhavantaṃ cārthopakāram anādṛtya), in-the-resort-of-the-own-form-made-service
(svarūpa-kṛtopakāra-samāśraya) — what grasp (graha) [is there]?

## The heart of the doctrine (the svātantryavāda)

Thus — since the whole shines (nirbhāsate), and in-which it shines-as-rested (viśrāntaṃ
nirbhāsate), and by-whose-will it shines (yadicchayā prakāśate), and which is-the-self of the
shining-whole (viśvasya prakāśamānasya ātmā), and which, in-the-whole-shining, [is]
uncovered (anāvṛta), and by-whose-freedom, even-being-non-excess, the whole shines-as-if-excess
— that [is] the supreme-reality (paramārtha-tattva), principal-ly (pradhānatayā) excellent
